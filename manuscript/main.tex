\begin{abstract}
Dynamic arrest is commonly pictured as a progressive slowing of motion. Small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) of the sequence-defined elastin-like polypeptide \VPAVG{} at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} resolves a different route. Over \SIrange{84}{131}{\minute} at that temperature, the fast-decay amplitude of a two-step correlation function falls from 0.38--0.70 to 0.00--0.13 across \SIrange{76}{167}{\nano\meter}, while the effective diffusion coefficient of that same fast mode changes by less than a factor of two. The mobile population therefore empties far faster than it slows, and the largest length scales arrest first. Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS resolves a reversible transition to a hierarchical assembly carrying $\beta$-sheet-like peaks at $Q=1.38$ and $0.72$~\Ainv{} that vanish on cooling. Arrest by local conversion rather than bulk stiffening implies that a partially arrested protein network retains fully mobile regions, which bears on solute transport and on the maturation of biomolecular condensates.
\end{abstract}

Amyloid-like $\beta$-sheet ordering occupies an unusual position in protein science and polymer materials. Pathological amyloid fibrils formed by proteins and peptides that share no sequence homology are associated with Alzheimer’s disease,\cite{Masters1985-pnas} Parkinson’s disease,\cite{Serpell2000-pnas} type II diabetes,\cite{Cooper1987-pnas} prion diseases,\cite{Pan1993-pnas} and systemic light-chain amyloidosis.\cite{Radamaker2019-ncomms} Many amyloid fibrils are kinetically persistent because extended hydrogen-bonded sheets are reinforced by tightly packed steric-zipper interfaces.\cite{Sawaya2007-nature} Yet amyloid-like order is not intrinsically pathological: functional amyloids store mammalian peptide hormones in pituitary secretory granules\cite{Maji2009-science} and build the extracellular matrix of bacterial biofilms,\cite{Chapman2002-science} and related assemblies are increasingly used as mechanically robust biomaterials.\cite{Knowles2016-am} What differs across these systems is not whether $\beta$-sheet-like order forms but how long it lasts: hormone granules disassemble to release functional monomer, whereas steric-zipper cores are far more persistent. Amyloid-like packing therefore spans a wide range of stabilities rather than committing a sequence to one permanent structure,\cite{Palmadottir2025-bpr} and where a sequence falls in that range is a materials-design variable.

Reversible $\beta$-sheet-like contacts are attractive as candidate physical junctions because backbone hydrogen bonding can stabilize locally ordered assemblies, whereas hydrated or weakly packed interfaces can remain dynamic or reversible.\cite{Kato2012-cell,Hughes2018-science,Luo2018-nsmb,Palmadottir2025-bpr} Elastin-like polypeptides (ELPs) are well suited to test this possibility because their sequence, molecular weight, and lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior can be programmed recombinantly.\cite{Meyer2004-zb,Amiram2011-la,Quiroz2015-mh,Varanko2020-ek,Saha2020-jg} Above the LCST, ELPs undergo hydrophobic desolvation and can form coacervates or hierarchical assemblies depending on sequence, concentration, and thermal history.\cite{Varanko2020-ek,Saha2020-jg,Sing2017-zq} In particular, alanine-containing ELPs can form stiff, thermoresponsive gels through arrested phase separation.\cite{Glassman2015-cw,Glassman2016-pq} Those studies establish the arrested state from time-averaged scattering and rheology. Neither can say how the assembly reaches it, and that is the question addressed here.

Whether an assembled state is still evolving is a question for a dynamical measurement. In colloidal gels, microscopic relaxation continues to evolve after the time-averaged structure has nearly stopped changing, a nonequilibrium process termed physical aging.\cite{Bahadur2019-ln,Chen2023-ho} Related heterogeneous relaxation has been resolved during protein liquid--liquid phase separation,\cite{Girelli2021-zz} protein gelation,\cite{Begam2021-fh} and cage relaxation in crowded protein solutions.\cite{Chushkin2022-bw} A protein-rich morphology can likewise keep reorganizing through junction exchange and residual coarsening while its SAXS profile changes modestly. A dynamical measurement can also separate two routes to arrest. If the assembly stiffens as a whole, relaxation times lengthen continuously. If it instead converts region by region, the material that is still mobile keeps its original relaxation rate while its share of the scattering falls. A bulk measurement averages the two together. Small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) probes the evolution directly through speckle decorrelation at the same low-$Q$ length scales sampled by SAXS.

Here, temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS and isothermal SA-XPCS provide complementary structural and dynamical views of concentrated aqueous \VPAVG, an ELP built from 30 repeats of the pentapeptide valine-proline-alanine-valine-glycine. At \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} the scattering is dominated by molecular dimensions with no detectable mesoscale assembly; heating to \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} produces hierarchical low-$Q$ scattering and high-$Q$ peaks compatible with $\beta$-sheet-like packing, and cooling restores the low-temperature profile. In a separate experiment on a freshly loaded sample, isothermal SA-XPCS at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} reveals a two-step decay whose fast fraction falls progressively with elapsed time. The mobile fraction therefore falls without the surviving dynamics slowing, while the local order that accompanies the assembly stays thermally reversible.

Figure~\ref{fig:static} establishes the low- and high-temperature structures and their reversibility. Two aliquots from the same stock were measured at APS 12-ID-B by simultaneous SAXS/WAXS at \SI{13.3}{\kilo\electronvolt}.\cite{APS12IDB,Xu2022-12IDB,Zheng2022-12IDB} The ``Measurement'' aliquot had undergone more than 10 heating/cooling cycles; the ``Reference'' aliquot had stayed at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius}. At \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} the two overlap, and a Guinier fit to the Reference gives an apparent $R_g=22.65\pm0.50$~\AA{} (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S3}), a dispersed state with no mesoscale aggregates down to $Q=0.0081$~\Ainv, that is below about \SI{78}{\nano\meter}. At \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}, both aliquots reproducibly develop an approximate $I(Q)\propto Q^{-3.2}$ response at low $Q$ (slopes $-3.17$ and $-3.15$ over $0.012<Q<0.040$~\Ainv). Both also develop the same pair of high-$Q$ features, at $Q=1.38$ and $0.72$~\Ainv{} in the Reference and 1.36 and 0.74~\Ainv{} in the Measurement. The cycled aliquot alone makes the point: it still develops both peaks at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} and shows neither at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius}. A more direct test at 8-ID-I took a single aliquot through seven consecutive \SIrange{6}{34}{\degreeCelsius} cycles, heated at \SI{1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S4}). Across the seven cycles the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} profile recovers to within a 9\% relative standard deviation with no systematic drift, and at \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} the correlation function stays close to $1+\beta$ in every cycle, the signature of an arrested state. The sample therefore assembled and arrested on every heating and returned to the same low-temperature state on every cooling (SI Section~\ref{SI-sec:results}). Video~S1 independently shows recovery of optical transparency after cooling.

The two reciprocal-space regimes describe a hierarchical assembly. Power-law small-angle scattering signifies density heterogeneity extending across the probed interval rather than a single resolved structural scale.\cite{Teixeira1988-jac} Related gel-forming ELPs exhibit disordered bicontinuous or fractal protein-rich networks produced by arrested phase separation.\cite{Glassman2015-cw,Glassman2016-pq} The 4.5 and 8.8~\AA{} spacings are compatible with strand-to-strand and sheet-to-sheet packing in cross-$\beta$-like assemblies, whose reflections are reported at approximately 4.8~\AA{} along the fibril direction and between 8 and 11~\AA{} across it.\cite{Sawaya2007-nature,Sunde1997-jmb} Both features are broad and exhibit full widths at half maximum of 0.25 and 0.34~\Ainv{} (correlation lengths $2\pi/\Delta Q$ of only about 25 and 19~\AA), so the ordering extends over a few repeat spacings rather than forming extended cross-$\beta$ crystallites. Their concurrent appearance with the low-$Q$ response establishes coexistence of local ordered contacts and mesoscale density heterogeneity. The disappearance of the peaks on cooling identifies thermally labile $\beta$-sheet-like associations that are plausible, rather than uniquely established, physical junctions.

To resolve mesoscale arrest at fixed thermal driving force, a freshly loaded sample was held at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} for more than \SI{30}{\minute}, warmed to \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{5}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute}, then to \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} to limit overshoot. Arrest is a kinetic crossover reached sooner the higher the temperature: the seven-cycle sample is still mobile at \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} and arrested within one ramp at \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S4}c), whereas at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} the fast fraction reaches zero only after about \SI{130}{\minute}. Colloidal gels behave the same way, the time to a given stage of arrest changing by two orders of magnitude over \SI{1.5}{\kelvin} of quench depth.\cite{Zhang2017-ip} The waiting time $t_w$ starts when the calibrated resistance-temperature detector (RTD) at the cell read \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}, and simultaneous SAXS and SA-XPCS then ran for \SI{7863}{\second} (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}). Each $t_w$ labels a group of averaged consecutive acquisitions at previously unexposed positions (SI Section~\ref{SI-sec:isoxpcs}). The SAXS intensity starts close to the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference and rises by more than two orders of magnitude at low $Q$ as $t_w$ advances, staying broad and monotonic (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}a). That \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference retains a weak low-$Q$ rise of its own, attributed to incompletely dissolved material in this loading (SI Section~\ref{SI-sec:coldstate}). This evolution shows increasing mesoscale density contrast without selection of a narrow domain spacing. Photon statistics, not a change in the sample's dynamics, set the ranges analyzed below. Before $t_w=5040$~s the low-$Q$ coherent intensity is too weak to resolve a two-step decay above the noise, and above $Q=0.00827$~\Ainv{} the scattered intensity has fallen by more than an order of magnitude (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}a) so $g_2$ is dominated by counting noise. Reliable correlation functions are therefore restricted to $t_w\ge5040$~s and the five lowest $Q$ bins.

The intensity autocorrelation function is
\begin{equation}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t)=\frac{\langle I(Q,t)I(Q,t+\Delta t)\rangle_t}{\langle I(Q,t)\rangle_t^2},
 \label{eq:g2}
\end{equation}
where $I(Q,t)$ is the coherent scattering intensity and $\Delta t$ is the delay time. Through the Siegert relation, $g_2$ reports the relaxation of density fluctuations at length scale $2\pi/Q$. At $Q=0.00376$~\Ainv{} (approximately \SI{167}{\nano\meter}), the correlation functions show a two-step decay: an initial millisecond-scale relaxation followed by an apparent plateau that persists throughout the \SI{2}{\second} acquisition (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}b). Resolving that first decay sets the frame rate. With $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ between 0.1 and \SI{1.6}{\milli\second}, a kilohertz acquisition would place the entire fast mode at or below its shortest delay and return only the slow component; the \SI{20}{\micro\second} frames used here sample it directly. With increasing $t_w$, the amplitude of the first decay decreases and the plateau rises. Such two-step relaxation and growth of a slow component are characteristic of heterogeneous kinetic arrest in protein and colloidal gels.\cite{Begam2021-fh,Bahadur2019-ln,Chen2020-fn}

The correlation functions were parameterized by
\begin{equation}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t)=1+\beta\left[
 f(Q)\exp\!\left[-\left(D_0\,Q^{2}\,\Delta t\right)^{p_{\mathrm{fast}}}\right]
 +\left[1-f(Q)\right]\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}(Q)}\right)^{p_{\mathrm{slow}}}\right]
 \right]^2,
 \label{eq:twomode}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is the instrumental speckle contrast, measured on a static reference and held fixed (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S7}), $f(Q)$ is the fitted fast fraction and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}(Q)$ an effective slow relaxation time. The stretching exponents carry no $Q$ argument because they are shared across the five bins of a global fit. $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is shared further, across every elapsed time as well: a Kohlrausch $\tau$ is comparable between fits only when they use the same exponent, since the mean relaxation time carries a factor $\Gamma(1/p)/p$.\cite{Guo2012-prl} One value serves the whole series, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=0.624\pm0.023$, and the fit is insensitive to it: $\chi^2$ per degree of freedom varies by under 1\% between 0.55 and 0.70. $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is left free at each elapsed time. The fast mode is written directly as a diffusive one, $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}(Q)=1/(D_0Q^{2})$, so the $Q^{-2}$ scaling is part of the model rather than something read off afterwards. Diffusion is the first form to try, and it describes the data: reduced $\chi^2$ is 0.77 to 1.90 across the series. A stretched correlation function is still diffusive as long as $p$ does not depend on $Q$ and $\tau$ goes as $Q^{-2}$, and both hold here. A Kohlrausch decay does not relax on $\tau$ itself, however, so the coefficient that belongs with a mean relaxation time is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{\langle\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}\rangle\,Q^{2}}
 =\frac{p_{\mathrm{fast}}}{\Gamma(1/p_{\mathrm{fast}})}\,D_0,
 \qquad
 \langle\tau\rangle=\frac{\Gamma(1/p)}{p}\,\tau,
 \label{eq:deff}
\end{equation}
which is the quantity reported below.\cite{Guo2012-prl} $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is fitted once for the whole waiting-time series rather than at each elapsed time separately, giving $0.595\pm0.023$. Freeing it per elapsed time changes reduced $\chi^2$ from 1.195 to 1.188 for four more parameters, an improvement significant only at $p=0.015$, and the $\chi^2$ profile is flat to within 1\% between 0.55 and 0.70: the data do not require five values. Holding it also keeps $D_0$ comparable between elapsed times, since $p$ and $\tau$ trade against one another in a Kohlrausch fit. $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is free at each elapsed time. The two-mode form describes the first decay and the persistent plateau without assigning either mode to a unique molecular mechanism. $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ exceeds the acquisition window, so it is an extrapolated descriptor; the primary quantities are $f$ and $D_{\mathrm{eff}}$.

The fitted fast fraction decreases monotonically with elapsed time at every measured $Q$. At fixed $t_w=5040$~s, $f$ increases with $Q$, from 0.38 at $Q=0.00376$~\Ainv{} (\SI{167}{\nano\meter}) to 0.70 at $Q=0.00827$~\Ainv{} (\SI{76}{\nano\meter}); by $t_w=7863$~s the same bins give 0.00 and 0.13 (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}c), so the complementary arrested fraction $1-f$ has gone from 0.62 and 0.30 at those two $Q$ values to 1.00 and 0.87 over approximately \SI{47}{\minute}. An increasing fraction of the scattering ensemble is therefore arrested within the accessible delay range, at every length scale probed. At every analyzed time $f$ also increases monotonically with $Q$, end to end across the five bins by 9.0 standard deviations or more (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}c and SI Section~\ref{SI-sec:xpcsfit}), so arrest develops first and most strongly at the largest length scale probed, \SI{167}{\nano\meter}, and progressively less so toward \SI{76}{\nano\meter}. That ordering holds within each elapsed-time group, so it reflects length scale rather than the trend with $t_w$, and it ties the growing low-$Q$ SAXS structure to the arrest fraction measured on the very same frames. XPCS does not locate the arrested regions, and $1-f$ is not a geometric volume fraction, because scattering contributions are weighted by structure and contrast.

What survives in the mobile population is ordinary diffusive material. $D_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises and then falls across the series, spanning $1.8$ to $5.3\times10^{7}$~\AA$^2$\,s$^{-1}$, ending a factor of 1.8 below where it started, a difference of 2.9 standard deviations (Figure~\ref{fig:xpcs}c). Progressive stiffening would look different: as regions tightened, their density fluctuations would relax more slowly and would stop relaxing diffusively. Instead the surviving fraction keeps a diffusive $Q^{-2}$ relaxation at a coefficient that moves by less than a factor of two while the fraction itself falls to zero. Material is therefore either still fluid or already arrested, with little in between. Across the same interval $f$ falls by a factor of five at the smallest length scale and to zero at the largest. Both the amplitude and the rate change, but the amplitude changes further and does so monotonically, so it is the amplitude that carries the arrest. A thermoreversible nanoparticle gel shows the same pairing of a plateauing diffusive fast time scale with a declining fast amplitude.\cite{Bahadur2019-ln} Both stretching exponents stay below unity at every elapsed time, the signature of a broad distribution of relaxation times rather than a single process; the product of $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ with the $Q$ exponent of $-2$ is $1.2$ rather than the value of 2 that subdiffusive motion of identical scatterers would give.\cite{Guo2012-prl} The fast amplitude and the fast relaxation time are not equally well determined at the last elapsed time: $f$ is set by the height of the plateau and remains measured, whereas $\langle\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}\rangle$ needs a resolved decay and is lost once $f$ falls to a few percent, so the lowest-$Q$ bin is dropped from the exponent there. $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is not used for mechanistic scaling because it lies beyond the acquisition window (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S8}). Correlation functions at the remaining $Q$ values are shown in Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S9}. Fresh material for every acquisition keeps cumulative dose from tracking $t_w$, so the trend in $f$ cannot be a dose artifact, and a control at 23--41 times this flux shows no change in $g_2$ with dose rate (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S10}).

The growth of low-$Q$ SAXS intensity and the decline of the fast fraction are two complementary aspects of network formation. This coupling is consistent with network-forming arrested phase separation in a dynamically asymmetric protein--solvent mixture, where concentration fluctuations build a connected dense phase and viscoelastic constraints suppress relaxation.\cite{Tanaka2000-qq,Girelli2021-zz,Begam2021-fh,Lu2008-nature} Related ELPs form bicontinuous networks with stress-relaxation times beyond $10^3$~s.\cite{Glassman2015-cw,Glassman2016-pq} The present measurements add a local structural element from a separate experiment: at high temperature \VPAVG{} carries thermally reversible $\beta$-sheet-like contacts. The separate experiments cannot place the ordered contacts at the junctions. They are nonetheless compatible in mechanism: arrest by discrete local conversion is the signature cooperative junction formation would leave, since a region locks when enough contacts form together rather than stiffening as each one tightens. $\beta$-sheet assembly is nucleated,\cite{Ferrone1999-me} self-amplifying once initiated,\cite{Knowles2009-science} and grows by templated addition onto a self-complementary interface.\cite{Sawaya2007-nature}

The route to arrest matters for what a partially arrested network can do. A medium that stiffens throughout retards every solute within it. One that converts region by region keeps pockets that are not merely less viscous but still diffusive, so transport should follow the connectivity of those pockets rather than a bulk viscosity, and release should hold and then fall at a threshold rather than tail off. Because the arrested fraction evolves in time and can be measured, a network interrupted at a chosen elapsed time carries a defined fraction, a handle that composition alone does not give. The same question is open for biomolecular condensates, whose maturation from liquid to solid governs their function and their role in aggregation.\cite{Banani2017-bb,Shin2017-hi} If maturation proceeds the same way, a partially matured condensate keeps interior regions that still exchange, and the liquid-to-solid transition is a connectivity threshold rather than a gradual drift. Condensates are too small and too dilute for the measurement reported here. A concentrated, sequence-defined mimic is not, and in this one arrest is local.

Four limitations bound the interpretation. The $\beta$-sheet-like assignment rests on WAXS peak positions rather than spectroscopy, for which attenuated-total-reflection infrared through a thermal cycle is the natural test, circular dichroism being poorly suited to a sample this turbid. The high-$Q$ ordering and the low-$Q$ dynamics come from separate experiments, so no time-resolved causal relation between them is established. The \SIrange{2e-5}{1.6}{\second} delay range quantifies the fraction arrested on that scale rather than the infinite-time nonergodicity factor, and linear shear rheology on a larger preparation is the natural complement. The isothermal series comes from one loaded sample, with fit uncertainties rather than between-sample variability, so the trend in $f$ is a single-preparation observation. Subject to those constraints, the combined data support network-forming arrested phase separation containing candidate, thermally labile $\beta$-sheet-like junctions.

In summary, SA-XPCS resolves how a sequence-defined protein polymer arrests. The amplitude of the mobile population falls by a factor of six at the smallest length scale and to zero at the largest, while its effective diffusion coefficient changes by less than a factor of two. Arrest therefore proceeds mainly by converting material out of that population rather than by slowing it, and it reaches the largest length scales first. SAXS/WAXS on the same construct resolves a reversible transition to a hierarchical assembly carrying $\beta$-sheet-like local order that vanishes on cooling. A protein network can therefore arrest region by region while the contacts that build it remain thermally erasable.

\textbf{Experimental Section.} The Sanger-verified construct encoded an N-terminal His-tagged \VPAVG{} protein (complete sequence in the Supporting Information). Recombinant \VPAVG{} was expressed in \textit{E. coli}, purified by immobilized metal-affinity chromatography, and buffer exchanged into \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES, \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} DTT at pH 8. Expression, solubility, and purity were monitored by SDS-PAGE (Figure~\ref{SI-fig:S1}b). Protein concentration was determined from $A_{280}$ using the sequence-specific extinction coefficient ($\varepsilon_{280}=1490$~M$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-1}$, 15{,}461~Da construct); the final concentration was approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}.

Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS measurements were performed at APS 12-ID-B with a \SI{13.3}{\kilo\electronvolt} beam of approximately $100\times150~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ and an incident flux of approximately $5\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$. At each set point the capillary was held at temperature for at least \SI{10}{\minute} before exposure. SAXS and WAXS were acquired simultaneously in \SI{0.1}{\second} exposures with an Eiger2 9M at approximately \SI{2}{\meter} and a Pilatus 300K at \SI{0.42}{\meter}. SA-XPCS was performed at APS 8-ID-I with a \SI{10.94}{\kilo\electronvolt}, $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ partially coherent beam and a Rigaku XSPA-500k detector \SI{7.8}{\meter} downstream. Each acquisition contained 100,000 frames at \SI{50}{\kilo\hertz} (\SI{20}{\micro\second} per frame, \SI{2}{\second} total). Further acquisition, fitting and radiation-control details are in the Supporting Information.
