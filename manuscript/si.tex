\section{Recombinant Synthesis and Purification of \texorpdfstring{\VPAVG}{(VPAVG)30}}

\subsection{Materials}
Shrimp alkaline phosphatase, PfuUltra II Fusion HS DNA polymerase, GoTaq Green Master Mix, and T4 DNA ligase with PEG4000 additive were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Exonuclease I was purchased from New England Biolabs. CircLigase ssDNA ligase was purchased from Lucigen, and KOD Hot Start DNA polymerase was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. T4 DNA ligase and CircLigase ssDNA ligase are distinct enzymes used in separate steps of the gene-construction workflow. PCR products and plasmids were purified using Monarch PCR and DNA Cleanup kits and Monarch Plasmid Miniprep columns. DNA was recovered from agarose gels using the Monarch DNA Gel Extraction kit. Oligonucleotides and the 5$'$-phosphorylated synthetic template used for rolling-circle amplification were obtained from Integrated DNA Technologies. \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold cells were obtained from Stratagene.

\subsection{Gene construction}
The repetitive ELP gene was prepared by overlap-extension rolling-circle amplification (OERCA).\cite{Amiram2011-la} A single-stranded DNA template encoding five VPAVG repeats was circularized in a \SI{20}{\micro\liter} reaction containing \SI{10}{\pico\mole} template, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ CircLigase buffer, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{1}{\milli\mole\per\liter} ATP, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MnCl$_2$, and 100 U CircLigase. The reaction was incubated for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, heat-inactivated for \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, purified, and treated with exonuclease I for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to remove unreacted linear template.

OERCA was performed with 300 ng circular template, \SI{1.5}{\micro\liter} each of \SI{50}{\micro\mole\per\liter} primers A1 and A2, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 70\% GC dNTP mix, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of \SI{25}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$, \SI{10}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ PfuUltra II buffer, \SI{5}{\micro\liter} DMSO, and \SI{2}{\micro\liter} PfuUltra II polymerase in a total volume of \SI{100}{\micro\liter}. Cycling consisted of \SI{2}{\minute} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}; 30 cycles of \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}, \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{54}{\degreeCelsius}, and \SI{90}{\second} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}; and a final \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}. Products above approximately 1.2 kb were isolated from a 0.8\% agarose gel.

Ligation-independent cloning adapters were introduced by PCR using primers B1 and B2 (Figure~\ref{fig:S1}), and the products were inserted into pMCSG53.\cite{Aslanidis1990-lm,Eschenfeldt2009-bi} Constructs were transformed into \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold, screened for soluble expression, and verified by Sanger sequencing. The selected construct encoded the amino-acid sequence \texttt{MHHHHHHSSGVDLGTENLYFQSNAV(VPAVG)30}, comprising an N-terminal affinity/fusion sequence followed by 30 VPAVG repeats.

\subsection{Protein expression and purification}
Fresh transformants were grown in Terrific Broth containing \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$ and \SI{300}{\micro\gram\per\milli\liter} ampicillin at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to an optical density of 0.6--0.8 at 600 nm. Cultures were cooled to \SI{18}{\degreeCelsius}, induced with \SI{0.2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} IPTG, and grown overnight. Cells were harvested at $5000\times g$, resuspended in ice-cold lysis buffer (\SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, 5 vol\% glycerol, \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES at pH 8.0, \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole, and \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} 2-mercaptoethanol), treated with \SI{1}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} lysozyme for \SI{10}{\minute}, sonicated, and clarified at $29{,}000\times g$.\cite{Makowska-Grzyska2014-cz}

The supernatant was applied to \SI{5}{\milli\liter} IMAC Sepharose High Performance resin, washed with lysis buffer, and eluted with lysis buffer containing \SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole. Purification fractions were monitored by SDS-PAGE. The purified protein was exchanged into \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES at pH 8.0, \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} DTT and concentrated using a 10 kDa molecular-weight-cutoff centrifugal concentrator. Protein concentration was determined by direct $A_{280}$ measurement on a NanoDrop spectrophotometer using the sequence-specific molar extinction coefficient applied during sample preparation. The final concentration was approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}; samples were stored at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius}, and freshly prepared protein was used for the X-ray measurements.

\section{Sample Environments and X-ray Measurements}

\subsection{Temperature-dependent SAXS and WAXS}
Two aliquots of \VPAVG{} at approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}, nominally the same concentration used for the 8-ID-I measurements, were sealed in \SI{2}{\milli\meter} quartz capillaries. The two beamtimes fell in different APS run cycles several months apart, and because the protein is purified fresh for each experiment, the 12-ID-B and 8-ID-I material came from separate preparations of the same construct. The aliquot designated Measurement underwent more than 10 heating/cooling cycles across the thermal transition, whereas the Reference aliquot remained refrigerated at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius}. Both aliquots were subsequently measured at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} and \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} during APS run 2021-1 at beamline 12-ID-B. The \SI{13.3}{\kilo\electronvolt} beam had an approximate size of $100\times150~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ and an incident flux of approximately $5\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$.\cite{Xu2022-12IDB,Zheng2022-12IDB} SAXS was recorded with an Eiger2 9M detector at an approximately \SI{2}{\meter} sample-to-detector distance, while WAXS was recorded with a Pilatus 300K detector at \SI{0.42}{\meter}. The Pilatus detector was mounted above the direct-beam path, allowing the transmitted beam to continue to the SAXS detector so that SAXS and WAXS were acquired simultaneously with a \SI{0.1}{\second} exposure. Buffer and capillary backgrounds were subtracted, and the SAXS and WAXS profiles were stitched over their common $q$ interval. At each set point the exposures were started as soon as the stage reached the target temperature, with no additional equilibration hold, so each profile reports the structure present within seconds of that temperature being reached. Because the two experiments used different sample cells, thermal paths, and thermometry, the 12-ID-B \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} profiles are not placed on the isothermal waiting-time axis of the 8-ID-I experiment; they are used here to establish the low- and high-temperature structures and their reversibility, not the kinetics of assembly.

\subsection{Isothermal SA-XPCS}
SA-XPCS was performed at APS 8-ID-I. Approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} \VPAVG{} in \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES (pH 8), \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} DTT was loaded into an aluminum cell containing a \SI{3}{\milli\meter}-diameter, \SI{2}{\milli\meter}-thick liquid chamber sealed by \SI{0.127}{\milli\meter} polycarbonate windows (Figure~\ref{fig:S2}). The cell was inserted into an anodized-aluminum holder in one temperature zone of the Quantum Northwest 3$\times$3 stage. Each zone was driven by a Peltier element and a resistive heater, and a calibrated resistance-temperature detector (RTD) embedded adjacent to the Peltier stage supplied the controller feedback and readout.\cite{Ozgulbas2023-oz} The aluminum holder and aluminum cell provided direct conductive thermal coupling. A partially coherent \SI{10.91}{\kilo\electronvolt} X-ray beam with 0.03\% FWHM bandwidth and incident flux $1.2\times10^{10}$ photons s$^{-1}$ was focused to approximately $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$. Coherent scattering was recorded \SI{7.8}{\meter} downstream with a Rigaku XSPA-500k photon-counting detector comprising 1024$\times$512 pixels with a \SI{76}{\micro\meter} pitch and a \SI{320}{\micro\meter}-thick silicon sensor. The detector was operated in 2-bit mode at a \SI{50}{\kilo\hertz} frame rate; each acquisition contained 100,000 frames, corresponding to \SI{20}{\micro\second} per frame and \SI{2}{\second} total duration. The multi-tau delay list is built from the recorded \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, and the longest accessible delay is \SI{1.638}{\second}.\cite{Zhang2021-jl}

For the isothermal experiment, the loaded sample was first held at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} for more than \SI{30}{\minute}. It was then heated to \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{5}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute}, followed by a slower ramp from \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} to the target temperature of \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute}. The two-stage protocol was used to reduce overshoot near the target temperature. The waiting time $t_w$ was set to zero when the calibrated RTD readout reached \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}; data collected during the preceding ramps were not included in the isothermal waiting-time series. SAXS profiles were obtained by temporal and azimuthal averaging. Each reported elapsed time is a group average, not a single acquisition: consecutive \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions were averaged in contiguous run ranges of 63--200 acquisitions after cross-correlation outlier rejection, and the quoted $t_w$ is the acquisition time of the first run in the range. The five analyzed groups begin at $t_w=5039$, 5969, 6595, 7221 and \SI{7863}{\second} and span \SIrange{7}{16}{\minute} each; because $f$ changes by less than its own group-to-group step within a group, this averaging does not materially broaden the correlation functions. Correlation functions were extracted at these elapsed times once the low-$q$ intensity provided sufficient photon statistics.

\subsection{Experimental replication and spatial sampling}
The isothermal SAXS/SA-XPCS sequence was acquired from one freshly loaded sample, and no independent sample replicate was collected. Each \SI{2}{\second} acquisition was recorded at a previously unexposed spatial position, providing repeated spatial sampling within the same preparation but not independent sample replication. The five $q$ bins probe different length scales within the same time series and likewise are not independent replicates. Uncertainties shown for fitted quantities are one-standard-deviation uncertainties propagated from the correlation-function errors through the nonlinear fit; they do not represent between-sample variability.

\section{SAXS/WAXS Reduction and Absolute-Intensity Calibration}

\subsection{12-ID-B SAXS/WAXS processing}
The beamline-reduced \texttt{.avg} files contained $q$, $I(q)$, and the corresponding intensity uncertainty. For each aliquot and temperature, the capillary-plus-buffer profile was subtracted using a factor of 1.00 for SAXS and the empirical factor of 0.95 used in the original WAXS reduction; sample and background uncertainties were added in quadrature. No absolute WAXS intensity is used in this work---only the peak positions and widths, which are insensitive to the value of that factor. The WAXS intensity and uncertainty were then multiplied by the ratio of the mean SAXS and WAXS intensities over their common $q$ interval, and the two profiles were concatenated and sorted by $q$. The uncertainty of this overlap scaling factor was not propagated.

For Figure~\ref{MT-fig:static} of the main text, the low-$q$ slope was obtained by an unweighted linear fit of $\log_{10} I$ against $\log_{10} q$ over $0.012<q<0.040$~\Ainv{} for the Reference aliquot at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}. The two WAXS features were fitted independently to a Gaussian plus linear background over $0.48<q<1.00$ and $1.18<q<1.70$~\Ainv, respectively. The reduced profiles and scripts implementing the background subtraction, overlap scaling, Guinier analysis, power-law fit, and peak fits are available in the public repository identified in Section~6.

\subsection{8-ID-I absolute-intensity calibration}
For the 8-ID-I data, the differential scattering cross section was calculated as
\begin{equation}
 I(q)=A_s I_s(q)-A_b I_b(q),
 \end{equation}
where $I_s(q)$ and $I_b(q)$ are the temporal-mean detector intensities from the sample and buffer, per pixel and per frame. The conversion factors were
\begin{equation}
 A_j=\frac{N_{\mathrm{f}}}{F_jT_jt_{\mathrm{exp}}L\Delta\Omega},\qquad j\in\{s,b\},
 \end{equation}
where $F_j$ is the incident photon flux, $T_j$ is the transmission, $N_{\mathrm{f}}=100{,}000$ is the number of frames, $t_{\mathrm{exp}}=\SI{2}{\second}$ is the total exposure time, so that $t_{\mathrm{exp}}/N_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, $L=\SI{2}{\milli\meter}$ is the sample thickness, and $\Delta\Omega=(d_{\mathrm{pix}}/D)^2$ is the solid angle per detector pixel.\cite{Sheyfer2020-yb} Lengths are carried in millimeters throughout, so $A_jI_j$ is in mm$^{-1}$; the factor of 10 to the cm$^{-1}$ of Figures~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a, \ref{fig:S6}b and \ref{fig:S8} is applied at the point of plotting.

Sample transmission was obtained from ion chambers immediately upstream and downstream of the sample. Across the attenuation series, the transmission of the intervening air path was independent of beam intensity within uncertainty, with a mean value of $0.8679\pm0.0009$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S3}a; mean $\pm$ standard deviation). The upstream ion-chamber signal was converted to incident photon flux by calibration against a PIN diode positioned in the flight path upstream of the beamstop. The measured photon flux varied linearly with the upstream ion-chamber readout (Figure~\ref{fig:S3}b). The absolute-scale SAXS evolution in Figure~\ref{fig:S8} used this transmission and flux calibration.

\section{SA-XPCS Correlation Analysis}
The intensity autocorrelation function was calculated using a multi-tau algorithm,
\begin{equation}
 g_2(q,\Delta t)=\frac{\langle I(q,t)I(q,t+\Delta t)\rangle_t}{\langle I(q,t)\rangle_t^2}.
 \end{equation}
The reduction produced 27 azimuthally averaged dynamic $q$ bins of nominal width $1.13\times10^{-3}$~\Ainv{} spanning \SIrange{0.00376}{0.03307}{} \Ainv. The five lowest bins, centered at $q=0.00376$, 0.00489, 0.00602, 0.00714, and 0.00827~\Ainv, are analyzed here. Both this $q$ restriction and the restriction to $t_w\ge5039$~s are signal-to-noise limits. The scattered intensity falls by more than an order of magnitude between the lowest bin and $q=0.00827$~\Ainv{} (Figure~\ref{fig:S8}), so above that bin the correlation function is dominated by counting noise; and before $t_w=5039$~s the low-$q$ intensity is itself too weak to resolve the second decay step. The temperature-ramp measurements of Figure~\ref{fig:S6}c show the still-mobile limit of that behavior directly. The centers correspond to $2\pi/q$ values of approximately 167, 128, 104, 88, and \SI{76}{\nano\meter}.

The two-step correlation functions were parameterized as
\begin{equation}
 g_2(q,\Delta t)=1+\beta\left[
 f\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}}\right)^{p_{\mathrm{fast}}}\right]
 +(1-f)\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}}\right)^{p_{\mathrm{slow}}}\right]
 \right]^2.
 \end{equation}
Here, $f$ is the fitted fraction of fast dynamics, $1-f$ is the fitted fraction of slow dynamics, $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are effective relaxation times, and $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are Kohlrausch--Williams--Watts exponents. $\beta$ is the instrumental speckle contrast, set by the transverse coherence of the incident beam and by the ratio of speckle size to detector pixel size; it is a property of the beamline configuration, not of the sample state, and therefore must not be allowed to vary with elapsed time or $q$. It was fixed at $\beta=0.13042$, with the baseline fixed at 1, and was measured on a static reference: a nano-porous glass standard of \SI{10}{\nano\meter} nominal pore diameter (\SI{10x10}{\milli\meter}, \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} thick), dataset F0145, recorded at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} about two hours before the isothermal series in the same beamline configuration. A static sample does not decorrelate, so $g_2(\Delta t)=1+\beta$. Averaging 50 repeat acquisitions at $q=0.02067$~\Ainv{} and fitting a straight line in $\log\Delta t$ gives a flat correlation function --- slope $-3.1(1.8)\times10^{-5}$, i.e.\ 1.7 standard deviations from zero --- with $\beta=0.13042\pm0.00001$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S4}a). That bin is not the best-counted one: each of the seven bins with smaller pointwise $g_2$ uncertainty departs further from flat, reduced $\chi^2$ ranging from 1.9 to 17.1 against 1.02 here. It is the best-counted bin whose averaged correlation function is flat to within its own uncertainties, and so measures the instrument rather than residual structure in the standard. Every one of the 50 repeats is statistically flat at this bin (worst reduced $\chi^2$ against a constant, 1.30), so none was rejected. Repeating the procedure bin by bin shows $\beta$ to be essentially independent of $q$ over the range used for the sample (Figure~\ref{fig:S4}b). Four of the five bins analyzed here in fact carry their own direct contrast measurement---$\beta=0.1297$, 0.1328, 0.1318 and 0.1319 at $q=0.00376$, 0.00602, 0.00714 and 0.00827~\Ainv, all within 2\% of the fixed value. The fifth, $q=0.00489$~\Ainv, is one of four bins out of 27 rejected by the flatness cut: there the standard returns $\beta=0.65$ with reduced $\chi^2=2346$, a detector or parasitic-scattering artifact rather than a contrast measurement, so no direct check is available at that $q$; the bins on either side bracket the fixed value at 0.1297 and 0.1328, giving no indication that the true contrast there differs. $\beta$ is flat at approximately 0.132 out to $q\approx0.021$~\Ainv{} and then declines steadily, reaching 0.106 in the outermost bin at $q=0.0331$~\Ainv. That decline is instrumental rather than a property of the standard: at \SI{10.91}{\kilo\electronvolt} the 0.03\% bandwidth transmitted by the Si(111) monochromator sets a finite longitudinal coherence length, and since the path-length difference across the scattering volume grows with scattering angle, contrast is progressively lost as $q$ increases. The reference bin used to fix $\beta$, $q=0.02067$~\Ainv, and all five sample bins, at $q\le0.00827$~\Ainv, lie below the onset of that falloff. Holding $\beta$ fixed across $q$ and elapsed time is therefore supported by direct measurement over most of the analyzed range. For each elapsed time, all five $q$ bins were fitted jointly with $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ shared across $q$; $f$, $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$, and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ were allowed to vary independently with $q$.

Fits were performed with \texttt{scipy.optimize.least\_squares} by minimizing the error-weighted residuals $[g_{2,\mathrm{model}}-g_{2,\mathrm{data}}]/\sigma_{g_2}$, using the pointwise $g_2$ uncertainties stored in the reduced HDF files. Initial values were $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=p_{\mathrm{slow}}=0.5$, $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}=10^{-3}$~s, $f=0.5$, and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}=100$~s. Bounds were $0.2\le p_{\mathrm{fast}},p_{\mathrm{slow}}\le3$, $10^{-6}\le\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}\le10$~s, $0\le f\le1$, and $1\le\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}\le10^4$~s. The maximum number of function evaluations was 40,000. Global fits with reduced $\chi^2\ge10$ were excluded from the plotted fit-parameter trends; no analyzed elapsed time meets that condition---the five give reduced $\chi^2=0.78$--1.89---so the cut removes nothing. The covariance matrix was estimated as the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse of $J^{\mathrm{T}}J$, with $J$ the Jacobian of the error-weighted residuals at the solution, so that a parameter refining to a bound (see above) does not render the uncertainties of the remaining, well-constrained parameters undefined; reported one-standard-deviation parameter uncertainties are the square roots of its diagonal elements. Power-law relations $\tau=Aq^{\gamma}$ in Figure~\ref{fig:S9} were obtained by weighted linear regression of $\ln\tau$ against $\ln q$, with $\sigma_{\ln\tau}=\sigma_{\tau}/\tau$.

The slow component remains only partially relaxed during the \SI{2}{\second} acquisition and frequently yields $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}>2$~s. It is therefore an effective extrapolation rather than a fully sampled terminal relaxation time. Conclusions regarding arrest are based primarily on directly observed features: the decreasing amplitude of the first decay, the rising plateau, and the systematic decrease in $f$, equivalently the increase in the slow fraction $1-f$. The monotonic rise of $1-f$ indicates that a statistically increasing fraction of the scattering ensemble is dynamically arrested within the experimental window. XPCS does not determine where those regions are located, and $1-f$ is not a direct geometric volume fraction because scattering contributions are weighted by structure and contrast. Because the measurement does not reach infinite delay, $1-f$ is also not identified with the formal infinite-time nonergodicity factor. Figure~\ref{fig:S9} reports all auxiliary fit parameters; Figure~\ref{fig:S10} shows the measured correlation functions and fits at the four additional $q$ values: the same two-step shape, and the same monotonic loss of the fast step with elapsed time, are present at every measured $q$, so the trend of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}c is a property of the whole \SIrange{76}{167}{\nano\meter} range and not of a single bin.

\section{Radiation-Damage Mitigation and Controls}
A Cartesian grid of $100\times100$ sample positions was defined in the aluminum cell, with adjacent points separated by at least one beam diameter. Every \SI{2}{\second} acquisition was collected at a previously unexposed position, preventing cumulative dose from tracking the elapsed time of the isothermal experiment.

An independent dose-rate test was performed after the APS Upgrade using a \SI{20}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} \VPAVG{} sample. The sample was held above the transition, at a stage-RTD readout of \SI{33.0}{\degreeCelsius} for every acquisition, so the control was recorded on an assembled rather than a dispersed state. Attenuation selected incident fluxes from $2.7\times10^{11}$ to $4.9\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$, approximately 23--41 times the flux used for the main experiment. The SAXS line shapes and $g_2$ decays showed no systematic flux dependence (Figure~\ref{fig:S5}). At this lower concentration the correlation function still decays essentially to the baseline within the accessible delay range, so the control samples an assembled but still mobile state and does not reproduce the arrested state of the primary experiment. Together with the lower protein concentration and the different beam configuration, this means the control tests for gross flux-driven artifacts rather than reproducing the complete main-measurement condition.

\section{Code and Reduced-Data Availability}
The reduced datasets and all analysis and figure-generation code used for this manuscript are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/qzhang234/PubCode-2021-IDPP}. The repository includes the reduced 12-ID-B SAXS/WAXS data, the averaged 8-ID-I XPCS HDF files for every group plotted, the per-acquisition temperature record of the thermal-cycling sequence, the individual correlation functions of the speckle-contrast standard, radiation-control data, absolute-intensity calibration inputs, and the scripts used to generate the main-text and Supporting Information figures. Every data-derived figure can be regenerated from the repository alone, and two further scripts, one per beamline, record how the committed reduced files---including the outlier rejection applied to each group, and the one group from which acquisitions were additionally removed by hand---were produced from the raw beamline results. The repository README maps each script and output file to the corresponding manuscript figure. Raw detector-frame datasets are not included in the repository and are available from the corresponding authors upon reasonable request.

\section{AI-Assisted Analysis-Code Development}
The Python scripts used for data processing, quantitative analysis, fitting, uncertainty propagation, and generation of data-derived figures were developed using Anthropic Claude accessed through Argo, Argonne National Laboratory's internal generative AI interface. The scripts read the experimental and reduced SAXS/WAXS and XPCS datasets; perform background subtraction, SAXS/WAXS stitching, absolute-intensity calibration, Guinier and peak fitting, global two-mode XPCS fitting, uncertainty propagation, and $q$-dependent scaling analysis; and generate the data-derived plots used in the manuscript and Supporting Information. The authors audited the scripts line by line, verified the analysis inputs and assumptions, reran the full analysis, regenerated all data-derived figures, and confirmed the reported numerical outputs. The scripts operate on measured or reduced data and do not generate experimental observations. All analysis choices, scientific interpretations, and reporting decisions were independently reviewed and approved by the authors.

\section{Supporting Structural and Dynamical Results}

\subsection{Macroscopic reversibility}
Video~S1 provides a direct visual demonstration of thermal reversibility. Brief warming of a capillary containing \VPAVG{} produces turbidity within approximately \SI{5}{\second}, indicating structures large enough to scatter visible light. After removal of the heat source, the sample returns to optical transparency in approximately \SI{60}{\second}. This recovery is consistent with the overlap of the cycled Measurement and refrigerated Reference SAXS/WAXS profiles at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius}. Optical transparency does not specify molecular structure, but independently confirms dissolution of the mesoscale assembly on cooling.

\subsection{Thermal-cycling repeatability of the SA-XPCS sample}
The reversibility comparison of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:static} sets two separate 12-ID-B aliquots against each other. A more direct test was made at 8-ID-I, where a single aliquot was taken through seven consecutive thermal cycles and compared with itself. This aliquot is not the loading used for the isothermal experiment of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs} of the main text: both are aliquots of the same stock, measured two days apart in the same cell position of the same nine-position temperature-controlled holder during the same beamtime, with the cell reloaded in between. The cycling sequence was: held at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, ramped to \SI{34}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1.01}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} over 270 consecutive \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions, then returned to \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, over a total of \SI{5.8}{\hour} (Figure~\ref{fig:S6}a). Sample temperature was read from the calibrated RTD of the stage zone holding the cell, once per acquisition. Every group averaged in Figure~\ref{fig:S6}---the seven \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} groups, the two high-temperature windows of each ramp, and the buffer---is first passed through the same cross-correlation outlier rejection used for the isothermal groups of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}, at the same 5th-percentile cutoff, and the surviving acquisitions are averaged. Typically 41--43 of 50 acquisitions survive, matching the 43 of 50 retained for the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference and the buffer of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}. One group is treated differently: eleven acquisitions of the fourth \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} group were removed by hand in addition, leaving 32 of 50. That was an author decision, taken by inspecting the individual curves of that group, and it is described and quantified below. Because no acquisitions were taken while the sample cooled, the temperature over those intervals was not recorded at all; in Figure~\ref{fig:S6}a it is shown dashed and is a projection from the fixed control protocol (cool at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} to \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, then hold), not a measurement. Every solid segment in that panel is measured, and the open symbols mark where each group plotted in (b) and (c) was taken.

Because every curve comes from one and the same material, any difference between cycles can only be irreversibility. Figure~\ref{fig:S6}b shows the buffer-subtracted absolute-scale SAXS profile at three states sampled in every cycle: \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} (the retained acquisitions of all 50 averaged), and two windows near the top of each ramp, acquisitions 241--250 (\SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius}) and 261--270 (\SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius}). Averaged over $0.004<q<0.008$~\Ainv, the seven cycles agree to a relative standard deviation of 8\% at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} and 5\% at \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius}, with no systematic drift from the first cycle to the seventh. The \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} profile still rises toward low $q$ rather than reaching the flat Guinier plateau expected of a fully dispersed solution. This is not mis-subtracted solvent: the buffer contributes only about 40\% of the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} signal at the lowest $q$, so removing the rise by rescaling it would subtract more than nine tenths of the low-$q$ signal and degrade the cycle-to-cycle agreement from 8\% to 25\%, while changing the \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} curves by less than 1\%. The rise is therefore left in. Its origin is identified below. The spread is larger, 33\%, at \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius}, and that is a sampling difference rather than a difference between cycles. Because the two high-temperature windows are taken on a ramp, each cycle crosses them at a slightly different temperature: acquisitions 241--250 fall between \SI{31.62}{\degreeCelsius} and \SI{32.48}{\degreeCelsius} across the seven cycles. That temperature lies on the steep part of the transition, and the logarithm of the intensity tracks the window temperature almost perfectly across the seven cycles ($r=0.974$, $\mathrm{d}\ln I/\mathrm{d}T=\SI{0.95}{\per\degreeCelsius}$); removing that trend reduces the spread from 33\% to 7\%. The \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} window lies past the steep part and needs no such correction (5\% to 1\%). The cycles therefore repeat to better than 10\% at every state once the temperature at which each was sampled is accounted for.

The \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} profile of Figure~\ref{fig:S6}b also falls off more steeply toward low $q$ than the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference plotted in Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a of the main text: log-log slopes of $-1.0$ and $-0.5$ respectively below $q=0.01$~\Ainv. The two are reduced identically---per-acquisition absolute scaling, the same outlier rejection, and the averaged buffer subtracted with no empirical coefficient---and over $0.005<q<0.032$~\Ainv{} they agree to within 18\%, departing only in the outermost bin, so the difference is confined to the lowest $q$ and lies in the acquisitions themselves. The per-acquisition distribution identifies it. Within a single \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} group of the cycling aliquot, and taking every acquisition as recorded, the intensity at $q=0.0035$~\Ainv{} is strongly right-skewed: relative standard deviations of 51\% and 96\% for two of the groups, with maxima 3.4 and 8.0 times the group median (skewness $+2.2$ and $+3.1$). The same acquisitions at $q=0.03$~\Ainv{} are symmetric with relative standard deviations near 9\%, that is, ordinary counting statistics. A long positive tail confined to low $q$ is the signature of a large object crossing the $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ beam in a minority of acquisitions. The isothermal aliquot of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs} does not show it (relative standard deviation 5.8\%, maximum 1.17 times the median). The cross-correlation rejection does not remove these acquisitions, because it compares curve shape over the whole $q$ range and a curve that is anomalous in three low-$q$ bins alone remains close to the group mean by that measure.

This is what the eleven hand-removed acquisitions are. They belong to the fourth cycle, the group with the 96\% spread, and every one of them exceeds 1.4 times the group median at $q=0.0035$~\Ainv. They were identified by eye from the individual curves and dropped before averaging; the mean convention used for Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs} is otherwise unchanged. The removal is not neutral, and is reported here rather than absorbed. Before it, the fourth cycle averaged 31\% above the mean of the other six over $0.004<q<0.008$~\Ainv; after it, 11\% below, and the cycle-to-cycle relative standard deviation at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} falls from 13\% to 8\%. Because the cut was applied to one group only, it is worth stating what a uniform cut would give: the same threshold of 1.4 times the group median flags between 6 and 13 acquisitions in each of the other six cycles, and removing all of them places the fourth cycle at the centre of the set rather than at either extreme, with a relative standard deviation of 10\% whose residual then comes from the sixth and seventh cycles. Neither variant changes any conclusion drawn from this figure, which rests on the ratio between cycles and not on their common level.

A difference between the two loadings survives all of this and is a property of the material rather than of a few acquisitions: the cycling loading has $I(0.004)/I(0.02)=4.0$ against 2.7 for the isothermal loading, and transmits 0.305 against 0.333. It was the less completely dissolved of the two.

Figure~\ref{fig:S6}c shows the corresponding correlation functions at the lowest $q$ bin. At \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} $g_2$ is flat at $1+\beta$ across the whole accessible delay range in every cycle, the signature of an arrested state, and independently confirms the contrast measured on the static reference. At \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} the correlation decays essentially to the baseline within the same window, so the assembly is still mobile there. This is also the direct evidence behind the elapsed-time restriction applied in the main text: while the sample is still mobile the low-$q$ correlation completes a single decay inside the accessible window, so there is no second, slower step there to resolve. No correlation function is plotted for the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} state. There the sample is a dispersed solution and the coherent signal at the lowest $q$ is too weak for any contrast to be resolved above the noise, so the measured $g_2$ carries no usable information; the two high-temperature states are shown precisely because they do. Both the contrast and the baseline are separately visible in the \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} curve---the condition under which it can be interpreted at all. Even at that temperature the per-acquisition statistics are poor: the curves in Figure~\ref{fig:S6}c are averages over ten consecutive acquisitions, and a single \SI{2}{\second} acquisition does not resolve the plateau.

\subsection{Low-temperature unassembled state}
The Reference profile at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} contains a Guinier regime satisfying $q_{\mathrm{max}}R_g=1.246$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S7}). A linear fit of $\ln I(q)$ against $q^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 R_g=22.65\pm0.50~\text{\AA}.
 \end{equation}
The fitted size is molecular in scale and is consistent with a dispersed, unassembled low-temperature state rather than persistent mesoscale aggregates. Because the protein concentration is high, this value is reported as an apparent $R_g$; weak intermolecular structure-factor contributions cannot be excluded. Recovery of the same low-temperature profile after repeated cycling establishes that the large high-temperature assembly is no longer detectable after cooling.

\subsection{Isothermal SAXS evolution at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}}
Figure~\ref{fig:S8} shows the full SAXS sequence. At $t_w=0$, the profile is close to the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference. The low-$q$ intensity then increases continuously and ultimately by more than two orders of magnitude, documenting progressive growth of mesoscale density contrast. The profile remains broad throughout, consistent with hierarchical network development rather than selection of a narrow domain spacing. The most pronounced increase occurs between $t_w=4088$ and 5039~s, after which the intensity continues to grow through 7863~s. This growth is what eventually brings the low-$q$ coherent intensity above the level at which a two-step correlation function can be resolved, and is therefore the origin of the $t_w\ge5039$~s restriction. The correlation analysis in Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs} of the main text uses $t_w=5039$--7863~s; earlier scattering was insufficient for reliable two-step correlations at the \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time. Over the $0.012<q<0.033$~\Ainv{} interval common to both beamlines---narrower at the top end than the $0.012<q<0.040$~\Ainv{} range behind the slopes quoted in the main text---the 12-ID-B \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} profiles give power-law slopes of $-3.09$ (Reference) and $-3.07$ (Measurement), whereas the isothermal 8-ID-I series steepens from $-2.45$ at $t_w=5039$~s to between $-2.64$ and $-2.68$ over 5969--7863~s. The isothermal series is thus still short of the structure reached by the 12-ID-B capillaries, and the two datasets are complementary rather than replicate measurements of one state.

\subsection{Length-scale-dependent relaxation parameters}
Figure~\ref{fig:S9} shows parameters from the global fits. Both shared stretching exponents remain below unity: $p_{\mathrm{fast}}\approx0.36$--0.70 and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}\approx0.26$--0.57. These stretched decays indicate broad relaxation-time distributions, consistent with a heterogeneous, nonequilibrium network.\cite{Begam2021-fh,Bahadur2019-ln}

The effective fast relaxation occurs on approximately $1\times10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-3}$~s and generally becomes faster with increasing $q$. At each elapsed time, the $q$ dependence was parameterized as $\tau(q)\propto q^{\gamma}$. The fast-mode exponent is close to $-2$ at every analyzed elapsed time, $\gamma_{\mathrm{fast}}=-2.0$ to $-2.4$. At the final elapsed time the fast amplitude at the lowest $q$ bin refines to $f=0.000\pm0.003$, i.e.\ no fast mode is detected there and $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is undefined; that bin is therefore excluded from the fast-mode panel of Figure~\ref{fig:S9} and from the corresponding power-law fit, which is why the final $\gamma_{\mathrm{fast}}$ carries a larger uncertainty. The fitted slow component also decreases with $q$, but its tens-to-thousands-of-seconds values exceed the acquisition window; neither $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ nor $\gamma_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is interpreted as a directly measured terminal relaxation law. The robust arrest measure remains the monotonic decrease in the fitted fast fraction $f$---equivalently, the increase in the fitted slow fraction $1-f$---across all five $q$ values.
