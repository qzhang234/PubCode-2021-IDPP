\section{Recombinant Synthesis and Purification of \texorpdfstring{\VPAVG}{(VPAVG)30}}
\label{sec:synthesis}

\subsection{Materials}
Shrimp alkaline phosphatase, PfuUltra II Fusion HS DNA polymerase, GoTaq Green Master Mix, and T4 DNA ligase with PEG4000 additive were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Exonuclease I was purchased from New England Biolabs. CircLigase ssDNA ligase was purchased from Lucigen, and KOD Hot Start DNA polymerase was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. PCR products and plasmids were purified using Monarch PCR and DNA Cleanup kits and Monarch Plasmid Miniprep columns (New England Biolabs). DNA was recovered from agarose gels using the Monarch DNA Gel Extraction kit (New England Biolabs). Oligonucleotides and the 5$'$-phosphorylated synthetic template used for rolling-circle amplification were obtained from Integrated DNA Technologies. \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold cells were obtained from Stratagene.

\subsection{Gene construction}
The ELP gene was prepared by overlap-extension rolling-circle amplification (OERCA).\cite{Amiram2011-la} A single-stranded DNA template encoding five VPAVG repeats was circularized in a \SI{20}{\micro\liter} reaction containing \SI{10}{\pico\mole} template, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ CircLigase buffer, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{1}{\milli\mole\per\liter} ATP, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MnCl$_2$, and 100 U CircLigase. The reaction was incubated for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, heat-inactivated for \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, purified, and treated with exonuclease I for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to remove unreacted linear template.

OERCA was performed with 300 ng circular template, \SI{1.5}{\micro\liter} each of \SI{50}{\micro\mole\per\liter} primers A1 and A2 (Figure~\ref{fig:construct}a), \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 70\% GC dNTP mix, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of \SI{25}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$, \SI{10}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ PfuUltra II buffer, \SI{5}{\micro\liter} DMSO, and \SI{2}{\micro\liter} PfuUltra II polymerase in a total volume of \SI{100}{\micro\liter}. Cycling consisted of \SI{2}{\minute} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}; 30 cycles of \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}, \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{54}{\degreeCelsius}, and \SI{90}{\second} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}; and a final \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}. Products above approximately 1.2 kb were isolated from a 0.8\% agarose gel.

Ligation-independent cloning adapters were introduced by PCR using primers B1 and B2 (Figure~\ref{fig:construct}a), and the products were inserted into the pMCSG53 vector.\cite{Aslanidis1990-lm,Eschenfeldt2009-bi} Constructs were transformed into \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold, screened for soluble expression, and sequence verified by Sanger sequencing. The selected construct encodes the amino-acid sequence \texttt{MHHHHHHSSGVDLGTENLYFQSNA(VPAVG)30}, comprising a cleavable N-terminal affinity/fusion sequence followed by 30 VPAVG repeats. That leader was kept rather than cleaved, because its single tyrosine is the only chromophore in the chain and the $A_{280}$ concentration measurement depends on it. Every measurement reported here is therefore on the tagged construct, and the leader accounts for 24 of the 174 residues and holds all eight charged residues.

\subsection{Protein expression and purification}
Fresh transformants were grown in Terrific Broth containing \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$ and \SI{300}{\micro\gram\per\milli\liter} ampicillin at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to an optical density of 0.6--0.8 at 600 nm. Cultures were cooled to \SI{18}{\degreeCelsius}, induced with \SI{0.1}{\milli\mole\per\liter} IPTG, and grown overnight. Cells were harvested at $5000\times g$, resuspended in ice-cold lysis buffer (\SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, 5 vol\% glycerol, \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfonic acid (HEPES) at pH 8.0, \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole and \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} 2-mercaptoethanol), treated with \SI{1}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} lysozyme for \SI{10}{\minute}, sonicated, and clarified at $29{,}000\times g$.\cite{Makowska-Grzyska2014-cz}

The supernatant was applied to \SI{5}{\milli\liter} IMAC Sepharose High Performance resin, washed with lysis buffer, and eluted with lysis buffer containing \SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole. Purification fractions were monitored by SDS-PAGE. The purified protein was exchanged into \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES at pH 8.0, \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} dithiothreitol (DTT) and concentrated using a 10 kDa molecular-weight-cutoff centrifugal concentrator. Protein concentration was determined from the $A_{280}$ measured on a NanoDrop spectrophotometer with $E^{1\%}_{280}=0.95$. All four loadings of the main experiment came from one batch, purified on 28 September 2021 and stored at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius} until the measurements in October 2021. Its $A_{280}$ of 9.60, normalized to a \SI{10}{\milli\meter} path, gives \SI{101}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}. The expression, solubility and purity of the protein were evaluated by SDS-PAGE (Figure~\ref{fig:construct}b).

\section{Sample Environments and X-ray Measurement Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{SA-XPCS at 8-ID-I}
\label{sec:isoxpcs}
Small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) was performed at APS 8-ID-I. \VPAVG{} at \SI{101}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}, in the same buffer, was loaded into an aluminum cell containing a \SI{3}{\milli\meter}-diameter, \SI{2}{\milli\meter}-thick liquid chamber sealed by \SI{0.127}{\milli\meter} polycarbonate windows. The cell was inserted into an anodized-aluminum holder in one temperature zone of the Quantum Northwest 3$\times$3 multi-sample temperature control unit, the same stage used in Ref.~\citenum{Ozgulbas2023-oz}. Each zone is driven by a Peltier element and a resistive heater, and a calibrated resistance-temperature detector (RTD) embedded adjacent to the Peltier stage supplies the controller feedback and readout. The aluminum holder and aluminum cell provided direct conductive thermal coupling. A partially coherent \SI{10.94}{\kilo\electronvolt} X-ray beam with 0.03\% FWHM bandwidth and incident flux $1.2\times10^{10}$ photons s$^{-1}$ was focused to approximately $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$. Coherent scattering was recorded \SI{7.8}{\meter} downstream with a Rigaku XSPA-500k photon-counting detector comprising 1024$\times$512 pixels with a \SI{76}{\micro\meter} pixel size and a \SI{320}{\micro\meter}-thick silicon sensor. The detector was operated in 2-bit mode at a \SI{50}{\kilo\hertz} frame rate; each acquisition contained 100,000 frames, corresponding to \SI{20}{\micro\second} per frame and \SI{2}{\second} total duration. The multi-tau delay list is built from the recorded \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, and the longest accessible delay is \SI{1.638}{\second}.\cite{Zhang2021-jl}

Every 8-ID-I measurement reported here follows the same acquisition pattern. Single \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions were taken one after another, each at a previously unexposed position on a grid of sample positions, at a cadence of roughly \SI{6}{\second} per acquisition once stage motion and readout are included. A single acquisition is therefore a snapshot of a fresh piece of sample at a known time, and a series steps through increasing time at that cadence. SAXS profiles were obtained by temporal and azimuthal averaging. Every reported quantity is a group average rather than a single acquisition: consecutive acquisitions were averaged in contiguous run ranges after the two outlier cuts of Section~\ref{sec:coldstate}, and the quoted time is the midpoint of the acquisitions in the range. The thermal protocol is given with its results in Section~\ref{sec:tdepdata}.

\section{8-ID-I Absolute Scattering Cross Section}
\label{sec:b8saxs}
\subsection{Absolute scattering cross section}
For the 8-ID-I data, the differential scattering cross section was calculated as
\begin{equation}
 I(Q)=A_s I_s(Q)-A_b I_b(Q),
 \end{equation}
where $I_s(Q)$ and $I_b(Q)$ are the temporal-mean detector intensities from the sample and from its background, per pixel and per frame. The background is a buffer where one was measured and the empty cell of Section~\ref{sec:tdepbg} otherwise. The conversion factors were
\begin{equation}
 A_j=\frac{N_{\mathrm{f}}}{F_jT_jt_{\mathrm{exp}}L\Delta\Omega},\qquad j\in\{s,b\},
 \end{equation}
where $F_j$ is the incident photon flux and $T_j$ is the transmission, both calibrated below (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}), $N_{\mathrm{f}}=100{,}000$ is the number of frames, $t_{\mathrm{exp}}=\SI{2}{\second}$ is the total exposure time, so that $t_{\mathrm{exp}}/N_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, $L=\SI{2}{\milli\meter}$ is the sample thickness, and $\Delta\Omega=(d_{\mathrm{pix}}/D)^2$ is the solid angle per detector pixel.\cite{Sheyfer2020-yb} Lengths are carried in millimeters throughout, so $A_jI_j$ is in mm$^{-1}$, and that is the quantity plotted in Figures~\ref*{MT-fig:xpcs}a and \ref{fig:T2800}--\ref{fig:T2875}: no further scaling of any kind is applied between the calibration and the figure.

\subsection{Transmission and incident-flux calibration}
Sample transmission was obtained from ion chambers immediately upstream and downstream of the sample. Across the attenuation series, the transmission of the intervening air path was independent of beam intensity within uncertainty, with a mean value of $0.8679\pm0.0009$ (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}a; mean $\pm$ standard deviation). The upstream ion-chamber signal was converted to incident photon flux by calibration against a PIN diode positioned in the flight path upstream of the beamstop. The measured photon flux varied linearly with the upstream ion-chamber readout over the seven attenuated settings of the series, to which the calibration line is fitted (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}b). The unattenuated point lies off that line and is excluded.

\section{8-ID-I SA-XPCS Correlation Analysis}
\label{sec:b8xpcs}
\subsection{Correlation functions and the fitted model}
The intensity autocorrelation function was calculated with a multi-tau algorithm,
\begin{equation}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t)=\frac{\langle I(Q,t)I(Q,t+\Delta t)\rangle_t}{\langle I(Q,t)\rangle_t^2}.
 \label{eq:si-g2}
 \end{equation}
\noindent The implementation is the 8-ID-I reduction workflow; its numerical treatment of the multi-tau scheme at these frame rates is described in detail elsewhere.\cite{Zhang2021-jl,Switalski2022-jsr}

The reduction produced 27 azimuthally averaged dynamic $Q$ bins of nominal width $9.9\times10^{-4}$~\Ainv{} spanning \SIrange{0.00359}{0.02937}{}~\Ainv, and 61 delay times from \SI{20}{\micro\second} to \SI{1.64}{\second}. The five lowest bins, centered at $Q=0.00359$, 0.00458, 0.00557, 0.00657 and 0.00756~\Ainv{} and corresponding to $2\pi/Q$ of approximately 175, 137, 113, 96 and \SI{83}{\nano\meter}, are analyzed here. The restriction is a signal-to-noise limit: the scattered intensity falls by more than an order of magnitude between the lowest bin and 0.00756~\Ainv, so above that bin the correlation function is dominated by counting noise.

The measured $g_2$ carry two decays separated by several decades in delay, and were parameterized through a normalized intermediate scattering function $g_1$ built from two stretched exponentials,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t,t_w)&=1+\beta\left|g_1(Q,\Delta t,t_w)\right|^{2},\\
 g_1(Q,\Delta t,t_w)&=f(Q,t_w)\,g_{1,\mathrm{fast}}+\left[1-f(Q,t_w)\right]g_{1,\mathrm{slow}},\\
 g_{1,\mathrm{fast}}&=\exp\!\left[-\left(D_0Q^{2}\Delta t\right)^{p_{\mathrm{fast}}}\right],\\
 g_{1,\mathrm{slow}}&=\exp\!\left[-\left(\Delta t/\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}\right)^{p_{\mathrm{slow}}}\right].
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:si-twomode}
 \end{equation}
The first line is the Siegert relation. The two-mode form on the second line is the same one used for thermoreversible nanoparticle gels, where the two-step decay is fitted as a linear sum of two stretched exponential modes with complementary amplitudes $a$ and $1-a$.\cite{Bahadur2019-ln} The same two-mode decomposition, with a fast decay and a slow stretched decay whose amplitude sets the intermediate plateau, describes ergodicity breaking in aging nanoclay suspensions.\cite{Chen2020-fn} Here $g_1$ is the normalized intermediate scattering function, $f$ is the fitted fraction of fast dynamics and $1-f$ its complement. $\beta$ is the instrumental speckle contrast, set by the transverse coherence of the beam and by the ratio of speckle size to pixel size. It is a property of the beamline and the detector rather than of the sample, is measured on a static reference, and is held fixed at 0.141 throughout (Section~\ref{sec:tdepbg}). $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is free at each $Q$ and each $t_w$. The fast relaxation time is not free, being tied to the diffusive form $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}(Q,t_w)=1/[D_0(t_w)Q^2]$, so one coefficient replaces five free times at each waiting time. Because the fast term equals $f/\mathrm{e}$ at $\Delta t=\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ for every value of $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is the $1/\mathrm{e}$ time of the fast decay and $D_0$ the diffusion coefficient that time implies, whatever the stretching. The Kohlrausch--Williams--Watts exponents carry no $Q$ argument because they are shared across the five bins. $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is free at each waiting time rather than shared across an isothermal experiment: these experiments run from a mobile state into arrest, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ falls from about 1 to about 0.2 as they do, and a single shared value is rejected by an $F$ test at $p<10^{-140}$ in every loading that arrests. The decomposition is phenomenological: it describes the observed first decay and persistent plateau without assigning either mode to a unique molecular mechanism.

\subsection{Global fitting and uncertainties}
\label{sec:xpcsfit}
Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode} was fitted to all five $Q$ bins of one averaged group at once. $f$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are free in each bin; $D_0$, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are single values shared across the bins of that group. Groups are fitted independently of one another, so nothing is shared across an isothermal experiment. Fits minimized the error-weighted residuals $[g_{2,\mathrm{model}}-g_{2,\mathrm{data}}]/\sigma_{g_2}$ with \texttt{scipy.optimize.least\_squares}, using the pointwise $g_2$ uncertainties stored in the reduced files, so each delay enters with weight $1/\sigma_{g_2}^2$ and the noisy short delays count for little. Bounds were wide ($0\le f\le1$, $1\le\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}\le10^4$~s, $0.2\le p\le3$, $10^{2}\le D_0\le10^{10}$~\AA$^2$\,s$^{-1}$). Each fit is started from the minimum of a $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ profile so that it cannot settle in a solution where the fast term imitates the slow one. Reduced $\chi^2$ runs from 0.90 to 3.34 over the four loadings, and a group is kept only if it stays below 10. Starting values and the remaining numerical settings are in the code (Section~\ref{sec:code}).

Parameter uncertainties are the square roots of the diagonal of the covariance matrix, estimated as the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse of $J^{\mathrm{T}}J$ with $J$ the Jacobian of the error-weighted residuals at the solution; the pseudo-inverse is used so that a parameter refining to a bound does not leave the uncertainties of the remaining, well-constrained parameters undefined. The fast fraction reaches its upper bound of one in 14 of the 170 bin fits, all of them in still-mobile groups of the isothermal experiments at 28.00 and \SI{28.25}{\degreeCelsius}; its smallest fitted value anywhere is 0.07. These are the error bars plotted in Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:xpcs}c and in Figures~\ref{fig:T2800}--\ref{fig:T2875}.

The slow component remains only partially relaxed during the \SI{2}{\second} acquisition and frequently yields $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}>2$~s, so it is an effective extrapolation rather than a sampled terminal relaxation time. Conclusions about arrest therefore rest on directly observed features: the decreasing amplitude of the first decay, the rising plateau, and the systematic decrease in $f$. Panels (d)--(g) of Figures~\ref{fig:T2800}--\ref{fig:T2875} give the correlation functions and fits at the four additional $Q$ values: the same two-step shape and the same monotonic loss of the fast step appear at every measured $Q$, so the trend of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:xpcs}c belongs to the whole \SIrange{83}{175}{\nano\meter} range and not to a single bin.

\subsection{Stretching exponents and the fast-mode scaling}
\label{sec:fastscaling}
Both stretching exponents stay below unity. $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ runs from 0.55 to 1.00 while a sample is still mobile and from 0.20 to 0.51 once it has arrested, and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is free as well. At $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=1$ the fast term falls from 90 to 10\% of its amplitude over 1.3 decades of delay; at 0.2 it takes 6.7 decades. That the breadth of the fast relaxation grows as the material locks up is itself a measurement rather than a modeling choice: a single $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ shared across an isothermal experiment is rejected by an $F$ test at $p<10^{-140}$ in each of the three loadings that arrest, while in the \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} loading, which does not arrest, freeing it moves the reduced $\chi^2$ only from 1.21 to 1.19. Stretched decays of this kind indicate a broad distribution of relaxation times rather than a single process, and are characteristic of protein and colloidal gels,\cite{Begam2021-fh,Bahadur2019-ln} although the direction of the departure from $p=1$ varies between systems: the egg-white gel of Ref.~\citenum{Begam2021-fh} relaxes with compressed rather than stretched exponentials, the signature of stress-driven rather than diffusive relaxation.

The fast relaxation occurs on approximately $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$~s and becomes faster with increasing $Q$. Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode} imposes the diffusive scaling $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}\propto Q^{-2}$ rather than fitting it, and a stretched $g_2$ remains compatible with diffusion provided $p$ does not depend on $Q$, which holds here by construction. Releasing the constraint, so that $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is free in every bin, returns a median $Q$ exponent of $-2.5$ over the 32 groups whose fast amplitude at the lowest $Q$ exceeds 0.15, with 80\% of them between $-2.6$ and $-1.3$. That release is also the general two-mode form of Ref.~\citenum{Bahadur2019-ln}, in which the fast time is free in every bin rather than carried by one coefficient. Refitting every group that way moves $f$ at the lowest $Q$ by 0.006 at the median, by 0.005 or less in the groups where the fast mode has nearly gone, and by at most 0.16 in a single group, and it moves the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest} by $-2.4\%$, against its bootstrap uncertainty of 9\%. Those fits are seeded as every other fit here is, from the minimum of a $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ profile. That matters, because with $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ free the two-mode form has local minima in which the fast term imitates the slow one: started instead from the bare default of 0.5, two of the 34 groups settle in one of them at reduced $\chi^2$ of 28 and 93, against 2.6 and 9.2 seeded, and the shift becomes $-0.3\%$. Both are small against that uncertainty. The collapse therefore does not rest on the diffusive constraint. The released exponents bracket the imposed value of $-2$ but sit systematically steeper than it, and in the later groups of the isothermal experiments in which arrest goes furthest they fall toward zero. The diffusive form is therefore reported here as an imposed approximation, not as a fitted result. What is read from it is the fitted coefficient $D_0$ of Section~\ref{sec:tdepfit}, and the conclusions of this work rest on $f$ rather than on $D_0$: $f$ is set by the height of the plateau and stays measured to the end of every isothermal experiment, whereas any fast-mode time scale needs a resolved decay. Freezing $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ at 0.5 for every group, against fitted values spanning 0.20 to 1.00, moves $f$ at the lowest $Q$ by at most 0.07 on a range of 0.07 to 1.00 and the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest} by $-3.4\%$, so neither result depends on how the stretching is treated.

\section{Radiation-Damage Mitigation and Controls}
\label{sec:radiation}
\subsection{Fresh sample positions}
\label{sec:freshspots}
The grid of positions referred to in Section~\ref{sec:isoxpcs} was Cartesian, $100\times100$ points in the aluminum cell, with adjacent points separated by at least one beam diameter. Because no position was visited twice, cumulative dose cannot track the elapsed time of the isothermal experiment, so the trend in the fitted fast fraction cannot be a dose artifact.

\subsection{Flux-dependence control}
In addition, to show that there is no significant radiation-induced artifact, an independent dose-rate test was performed after the APS Upgrade using a \SI{20}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} \VPAVG{} sample. Every acquisition was taken at a stage-RTD readout of \SI{33.0}{\degreeCelsius}, above the transition temperature, so the control probed the assembled high-temperature state rather than the low-temperature dispersed one. Attenuation selected incident fluxes from $2.7\times10^{11}$ to $4.9\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$, approximately 22--41 times the flux used for the main experiment. The SAXS line shapes and $g_2$ decays showed no systematic flux dependence (Figure~\ref{fig:flux}). That control sample is five times more dilute than the \SI{101}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} material of the main experiment, and it was measured with the post-Upgrade beam. The main series is protected instead by the fresh position used for every acquisition (Section~\ref{sec:freshspots}).

\subsection{Outlier rejection}
\label{sec:coldstate}
Two cuts are applied to every group in this work on identical terms, with no per-group list. The first compares each acquisition with the group on the shape of $g_2$, $g_2$ uncertainty and $I(Q)$ by cross-correlation, and drops the lowest fifth percentile of each similarity score; being a percentile rather than a threshold, it removes 10 to 15\% of every group whatever the data look like. The second is robust rather than fractional and catches what the first does not: a large object drifting through the $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ beam during a minority of acquisitions raises $I(Q)$ in the lowest few $Q$ bins alone, which barely moves a whole-profile shape score. For each acquisition the mean of $I(Q)$ over $0.004<Q<0.008$~\Ainv{} is compared with the group median, and the acquisition is rejected if it exceeds that median by more than three robust standard deviations, the scale being $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation, both statistics iterated to convergence. The test is one-sided because a transient scatterer only adds intensity. The threshold is not tuned: the same acquisitions are selected for any value between 2.5 and 3 and for two other choices of band.

\section{Temperature Dependence of Arrest}
\label{sec:tdep}
\subsection{Measurements and reduction}
\label{sec:tdepdata}
The four loadings that carry the main text were measured at 8-ID-I in October 2021, with the beam energy, detector, detector distance and cell design of Section~\ref{sec:isoxpcs}. After at least \SI{30}{\minute} of equilibration and a reference measurement at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, each loading was warmed to \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} and heated from there at \SI{0.1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} to its final temperature, where it was held: loading D8 at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius}, D6 at \SI{28.25}{\degreeCelsius}, D7 at \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius} and D9 at \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}. The temperatures are the readings of the stage RTD, and $t_w=0$ is the first acquisition with the RTD at its target. The groups are cut so that one begins at $t_w=0$, and only groups recorded at the target are fitted. Acquisitions of 100,000 frames ran at fresh positions through the ramp and then through isothermal experiments of 7100, 8500, 3900 and \SI{2200}{\second}, respectively.

The 2021 Data Exchange records were converted to the NeXus format with the incident- and transmitted-beam monitors and the ring current carried across unchanged, and the frames were correlated with the 8-ID-I reduction workflow of Section~\ref{sec:b8xpcs}. Consecutive acquisitions were averaged in groups of 100, each spanning about \SI{10}{\minute}, with one group boundary at $t_w=0$. Each group passed the same two outlier cuts as every other group in this work (Section~\ref{sec:coldstate}) and is placed at the midpoint of the acquisitions it contains. One detector pixel read a constant 1.5 photons per frame throughout the measurements reported here and is masked in the $Q$ map the series is correlated against. It lay at $Q=0.0214$~\Ainv, outside the five $Q$ bins of the correlation analysis; masking it left every fitted quantity unchanged.

\subsection{Absolute scale, background and contrast}
\label{sec:tdepbg}
The beamtime used the same ion chambers as the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:b8saxs}, so $I(Q)$ is put on that absolute scale, each group with its own monitor readings. The four loadings transmit 0.383 to 0.389. The background is a separate measurement of the sample cell, subtracted on the absolute scale exactly as a buffer would be. Six candidate measurements are available, four of them recorded on the same day as the loadings. They are identified here by what they transmit and what they scatter rather than by the labels entered at the beamline, which do not reliably distinguish a cell left empty from one holding buffer. Four of the six recorded a transmitted-beam monitor. One of those passes 0.94 of the beam and the other three pass 1.00, the same as the open path, so one has of order 6\% of attenuating material in the beam and the others have none that the chambers resolve. None passes anything near 0.41, which is what a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} column of buffer behind the same material would give, so none of them holds a liquid column of the sample thickness. The measurement that passes 0.94 is accordingly the only one of the six that carries scattering from material in the beam path, and that is what the subtraction needs. Fitted by the procedure below, the three that pass 1.00 need a coefficient between 3 and 5 and still leave the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} references 7 to 28\% from flat at best, against 3 to 5\% for the one that passes 0.94, with the residual peaking near 0.008~\Ainv. Averaging the three together does not improve on the best single one, so what they lack is shape and not counting statistics. Whether any of these measurements held buffer changes little over this $Q$ range: the absorption difference is taken up by the coefficient, and the scattering of a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} water column is about 0.0016~mm$^{-1}$, some 6\% of the plateau and flat in $Q$, so it shifts the level rather than the shape.

How much of that background to subtract is set by the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} state, which is dissolved and whose profile should fall smoothly. Each reference is reduced to the median in each of nine logarithmic $Q$ bins between 0.0036 and 0.0305~\Ainv, and two numbers describe the result: the rise, its lowest bin divided by its highest, and the largest fractional increase from one bin to the next going up in $Q$, which is zero when the profile never turns back up. The coefficient trades one against the other and nothing in these data escapes that trade. At $\alpha=0.865$, the value that makes the references flattest, the rise is 1.0 but the profile dips 6 to 8\% near 0.004 to 0.005~\Ainv, and a dip is a subtraction artifact rather than anything the sample does. Removing the dip altogether takes $\alpha=0.60$, which leaves a two-fold climb toward low $Q$ in its place. We use $\alpha=0.80$ for all four loadings. The rise is then 1.26 to 1.45 and the profile turns back up by at most 4\%, below the scatter of the individual points, and the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} references sit at 0.026 to 0.028~mm$^{-1}$ over 0.006 to 0.03~\Ainv.

Figures~\ref{fig:T2800}--\ref{fig:T2875} show $I(Q)$ from the lowest fitted $Q$ bin outward. Below that bin the points sit on the edge of the beamstop shadow, where the background and the sample no longer share a profile. As the transmissions above show, none of this beamtime's background measurements holds a buffer column, so the subtraction cannot take windows, solvent and parasitic scattering off together the way a buffer measured in the same cell does. At 0.004~\Ainv{} the background removed is 2.4 to 3.0 times the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} signal left behind, so subtracting enough to remove the instrumental tail would take the sample's own low-$Q$ signal with it, and the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} references keep a residual rise at every coefficient. A mismatch of the $Q$ scale between the two was tested as the cause and rejected: flattening the residual that way takes a 6\% stretch of the background $Q$ axis and improves the agreement by half a percentage point. Averaged over 0.004 to 0.008~\Ainv, the 34 fitted groups stand 54 to 322 times the background, so this limit bears on the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference alone.

Loading D8 was measured at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} both before and after its isothermal experiment, and Figure~\ref{fig:T2800} shows the later measurement. The earlier one was the first measurement of the beamtime and is not a dissolved state: it carries a $Q^{-2.7}$ excess at low $Q$ that falls to 4\% of the plateau above 0.01~\Ainv, and it fits $\alpha=1.15$ against 0.85 to 0.88 for every other reference. The later measurement is the same loading in the same cell, returned to \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} after the isothermal experiment at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius}; it is flat and fits $\alpha=0.860$. The excess is therefore undissolved material in the earlier measurement rather than a property of the subtraction.

The speckle contrast was measured on a static reference recorded in the same beamtime, as the flat level of its correlation function through the Siegert relation that opens Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode}. A static sample cannot relax, so each of its correlation functions is flat in delay and sits at $1+\beta$. The value is \emph{not} taken over the five bins the correlation analysis fits, where that reference reads 0.1226, 0.1323, 0.1355, 0.1372 and 0.1390 with increasing $Q$: the standard is scratched, and its static parasitic scattering depresses the apparent contrast most strongly at low $Q$. $\beta$ is instead read from the plateau the same reference reaches between 0.0085 and 0.0165~\Ainv, where nine consecutive bins give 0.1379 to 0.1428 and their inverse-variance mean is $\beta=0.141$, the single value used for every bin and every group. Using the fitted bins instead would understate $\beta$ by 5\%, which returns too large an $f$: the arrested fractions quoted below would rise from 0.93 to 0.96, the ordering of $f$ in $Q$ would be unchanged, and the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest} would move by $-1.4\%$.

\subsection{Fitting}
\label{sec:tdepfit}
Equation~\ref*{MT-eq:twomode} of the main text was fitted to the five lowest $Q$ bins, 0.00359 to 0.00756~\Ainv{} (175 to \SI{83}{\nano\meter}), with $\beta$ fixed. A group is fitted when its correlation function is no noisier than a fixed threshold: the median $g_2$ uncertainty at the lowest $Q$ between $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-2}$~s may not exceed 0.016. Two choices follow from what the isothermal experiments contain. First, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is fitted in each group. The experiments run from a largely mobile state, where $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is 0.55 to 1.00, into arrest, where it is 0.20 to 0.51, and a single value shared across an experiment is rejected by an $F$ test in every loading, at $p<10^{-140}$ in the three that arrest. Each fit starts from the minimum of a $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ profile, so that it cannot settle in a solution in which the fast term imitates the slow one. Second, the rate of the fast mode is reported as the fitted coefficient $D_0$, the diffusion coefficient implied by the $1/\mathrm{e}$ time of the fast decay. That time does not depend on $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$, so $D_0$ compares like with like between groups whose stretching differs. Where $f$ at the lowest $Q$ is below 0.25 the fast decay is too weak to fix a rate, and $D_0$ is shown but not used. Reduced $\chi^2$ is 0.90 to 3.3. Early in arrest $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ reaches its upper bound of $10^4$~s in 17 of 170 bin fits, where the slow mode does not decay within the acquisition; $f$ is set by the height of the plateau and is unaffected.

Fixing $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ would remove one parameter per group, and the data were tested against it. At $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=1$, where the fast mode is a single exponential, the mean reduced $\chi^2$ of the isothermal experiment at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} rises only from 1.18 to 1.41, so a mobile sample does relax exponentially. In the three experiments in which the sample arrests, it rises from 1.75, 1.89 and 1.64 to 8.27, 9.56 and 7.22, and the fitted curve crosses the data rather than following it. Intermediate fixed values do not recover the fit: averaged over all 34 groups, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=1$ gives 6.60, 0.7 gives 3.73 and 0.5 gives 2.44, against 2.21 for the best single value shared within each isothermal experiment and 1.62 with $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ fitted per group. The broadening of the fast relaxation is therefore a feature of the data wherever the sample arrests rather than a free parameter absorbing noise.

\subsection{Results}
Figures~\ref{fig:T2800}--\ref{fig:T2875} show the four loadings in the format of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:xpcs} of the main text, with the correlation functions at the four further $Q$ values in panels (d)--(g). Assembly begins before any target is reached. Averaged over $0.004<Q<0.008$~\Ainv, the scattered intensity rises on the ramp to 2.3 to 3.6 times its level at 20 to \SI{22}{\degreeCelsius} by \SI{27.5}{\degreeCelsius}, in every loading.

At \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} the sample assembles without arresting. The same low-$Q$ intensity grows 16-fold during the isothermal experiment, to 5.8~mm$^{-1}$, while $f$ stays at 0.89 or above at every $Q$ throughout.

At the three higher temperatures the sample arrests, and the rate rises steeply with temperature. $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5 after 6100, 2220 and \SI{830}{\second} at 28.25, 28.50 and \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}, a factor of 7.3 over \SI{0.5}{\degreeCelsius}, against $\exp(0.5\,\mathrm{K}/T_s)=7.8$ for the scaling temperature of Section~\ref{sec:scaling}. In every group in which arrest is under way ($f<0.85$ at the lowest $Q$), $f$ increases monotonically with $Q$: 7 of 7, 7 of 7 and 4 of 4 groups. Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest} puts $f$ at the largest length scale for all four isothermal experiments on one axis. At \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius} the last group gives $f=0.07$ at \SI{175}{\nano\meter} and 0.16 at \SI{83}{\nano\meter}.

Structure and arrest advance along the same clock (Section~\ref{sec:scaling}). In each sample that arrests, $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5 when the low-$Q$ intensity reaches 7.2 to 8.3~mm$^{-1}$, and the isothermal experiment at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} ends at 5.8~mm$^{-1}$ with $f=0.89$. Once under way, arrest proceeds while the low-$Q$ intensity keeps rising, by a factor of 1.7 to 4.0 between the first group in which arrest is under way and the last, and by 1.2 to 1.7 after half arrest.

The mobile population slows before arrest and then changes far less than the amplitude does (Figure~\ref{fig:fastmode}). While $f$ at the lowest $Q$ stays at 0.7 or above, $D_0$ falls 2.7-, 3.8- and 3.4-fold at 28.00, 28.25 and \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius}. Below 0.7, and as long as $f\geq0.25$ so that the fast decay fixes a rate, it varies by factors of 1.7 and 1.3 at 28.25 and \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius}, with no trend, while $f$ falls from 0.67 to 0.29 and from 0.54 to 0.37. Below 0.25 the amplitude keeps falling, to 0.14, 0.07 and 0.16 at the three arresting temperatures. At those three temperatures $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ falls from 0.7--0.9 in the first group to 0.20--0.35 in the last.

\subsection{Scaling analysis}
\label{sec:scaling}
The fast fraction at the lowest $Q$ of all four isothermal experiments, one value from each of 34 groups, is fitted with one master curve,
\begin{equation}
 f=\frac{A}{1+(X/X_c)^{n}},\qquad X=t_w\exp\!\left(\frac{T-T_0}{T_s}\right),
 \label{eq:si-master}
\end{equation}
weighting each point by its fit uncertainty floored at 0.01. The fit returns $T_s=0.243$~K, $X_c=18\,600$~s, $n=3.3$ and $A=0.92$, and 200 bootstrap resamples of the groups give $T_s$ an uncertainty of $\pm0.021$~K. The rescaling improves the residual threefold over no rescaling, to 8.2 fit uncertainties, so the collapse is approximate. Collapsed the same way one bin at a time, $T_s$ is 0.243, 0.247, 0.252, 0.260 and 0.267~K across the five bins, each with a bootstrap uncertainty of 0.02~K (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}i). At the $T_s$ of the lowest bin, the characteristic time $X_c$ rises from 18\,600 to 23\,800~s across the five bins (Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}j), so the smaller length scales arrest about 1.3 times later.

The low-$Q$ intensity, the mean absolute $I(Q)$ of each group over 0.004 to 0.008~\Ainv, is placed on $t_w\exp[(T-T_0)/T_s]$ with the $T_s$ above (Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest}c). A cubic in $\log I$ against the logarithm of that variable, fitted to all four isothermal experiments, returns 0.24~K as the $T_s$ that superposes the intensity on its own, the value above, and at that value the scatter about one curve is 13 times smaller than without rescaling. Every group is used, including the early groups at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius}, whose correlation functions are too noisy to fit. In each sample that arrests, $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5 at 175 to 220 times its low-$Q$ intensity at \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius}.

The band and the half-arrest point are the two choices behind Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest}c. The band, 0.004 to 0.008~\Ainv, spans the length scales of the five $Q$ bins of the correlation analysis, and it is where the intensity grows most: over the isothermal experiment at \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius}, $I(Q)$ rises 5.2-fold at 0.004~\Ainv, 3.0-fold at 0.008~\Ainv{} and 2.1-fold at 0.02~\Ainv. Other bands superpose at the same scaling temperature. Fitted on its own as above, the mean intensity over 0.0031 to 0.0081~\Ainv, the full width of the five bins, returns 0.24~K, as do 0.005 to 0.010 and 0.008 to 0.016~\Ainv; 0.012 to 0.030~\Ainv{} returns 0.25~K. The scatter about one curve falls 14-, 12-, 9- and 6-fold against no rescaling, less at higher $Q$, where the intensity changes less. Half arrest is the waiting time at which $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5, interpolated linearly between the two groups that bracket it. Other thresholds give the same picture. For thresholds of 0.3, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.7, the three samples that arrest cross them at 5.7 to 9.2~mm$^{-1}$, and the intensity grows 1.1- to 1.9-fold after the crossing to the end of each isothermal experiment.

\subsection{Activation energy and bond number}
\label{sec:bondnumber}
\textbf{Activation energy.} In the collapse of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest}b, $f$ depends on $t_w$ and $T$ only through $X=t_w\exp[(T-T_0)/T_s]$. A given value of $f$ is therefore reached at a fixed $X_f$, after a waiting time
\begin{equation}
 t_w(T)=X_f\exp\!\left(-\frac{T-T_0}{T_s}\right).
 \label{eq:si-tw}
\end{equation}
The rate of arrest, $k\propto1/t_w$, then changes with temperature as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathrm{d}\ln k}{\mathrm{d}T}=\frac{1}{T_s}.
 \label{eq:si-slope}
\end{equation}
For an Arrhenius rate, $k=A\exp(-E_a/k_\mathrm{B}T)$ with a constant prefactor $A$, the same derivative is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathrm{d}\ln k}{\mathrm{d}T}=\frac{E_a}{k_\mathrm{B}T^2}.
 \label{eq:si-arrhenius}
\end{equation}
Equating Equations~\ref{eq:si-slope} and \ref{eq:si-arrhenius} gives
\begin{equation}
 E_a=\frac{k_\mathrm{B}T^2}{T_s}.
 \label{eq:si-ea}
\end{equation}
Across the 0.75~K spanned here $1/T$ changes by only 0.25\%, so the exponential rescaling and the Arrhenius form describe the data equally well. At the mean temperature, $T=301.5$~K, $T_s=0.243\pm0.021$~K gives $E_a=(1.24\pm0.11)\times10^{3}\,k_\mathrm{B}T$, or $3.11\pm0.27$~MJ/mol, the uncertainty carried from $T_s$.

\textbf{Transition-state theory.} The rate of a thermally activated step is
\begin{align}
 k&=\frac{k_\mathrm{B}T}{h}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta G^\ddagger}{k_\mathrm{B}T}\right),\label{eq:si-eyring}\\
 \Delta G^\ddagger&=\Delta H^\ddagger-T\Delta S^\ddagger,\label{eq:si-dg}
\end{align}
where $h$ is Planck's constant and $\Delta G^\ddagger$, $\Delta H^\ddagger$ and $\Delta S^\ddagger$ are the free energy, enthalpy and entropy of activation. Taking the derivative of $\ln k$, with $\Delta H^\ddagger$ and $\Delta S^\ddagger$ constant over the narrow range,
\begin{equation}
 E_a=k_\mathrm{B}T^2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln k}{\mathrm{d}T}=\Delta H^\ddagger+k_\mathrm{B}T.
 \label{eq:si-dh}
\end{equation}
The Arrhenius slope therefore gives the enthalpy of activation, $\Delta H^\ddagger=3.11$~MJ/mol, but not the entropy. The free energy of activation follows from the rate itself. Taking the waiting time $t_{1/2}$ at which $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5 as $1/k$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta G^\ddagger=k_\mathrm{B}T\,\ln\!\left(\frac{k_\mathrm{B}T}{h}\,t_{1/2}\right).
 \label{eq:si-dgnum}
\end{equation}
For $t_{1/2}=6100$, 2220 and \SI{830}{\second} at 28.25, 28.50 and \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}, $\Delta G^\ddagger$ is 38.2, 37.2 and 36.2~$k_\mathrm{B}T$, or 96, 93 and 91~kJ/mol. The entropy of activation is then
\begin{equation}
 T\Delta S^\ddagger=\Delta H^\ddagger-\Delta G^\ddagger=3.02~\mathrm{MJ\,mol^{-1}},
 \label{eq:si-ds}
\end{equation}
or $\Delta S^\ddagger=10.0$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$, which offsets 97\% of the activation enthalpy. The estimate assumes the molecular prefactor $k_\mathrm{B}T/h$. A prefactor $10^{6}$ times smaller would lower $\Delta G^\ddagger$ by 13.8~$k_\mathrm{B}T$ and raise the compensation to 98\%.

\textbf{Evolution and relaxation.} $E_a$ belongs to the evolution toward arrest, not to the relaxation at a given stage. At the lowest $Q$ the fast relaxation has the $1/\mathrm{e}$ time
\begin{equation}
 \tau_\mathrm{fast}=\frac{1}{D_0Q^2},
 \label{eq:si-taufast}
\end{equation}
and a stage $X$ of the master curve is reached after the waiting time of Equation~\ref{eq:si-tw}. The fast-mode coefficient $D_0$ is interpolated between the groups that bracket each $X$, among those with $f\geq0.25$ at the lowest $Q$, where the fast decay fixes a rate. At $X=3000$~s, $\tau_\mathrm{fast}$ is 1.18, 1.25 and 1.28~ms at 28.00, 28.25 and \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius}, reached after 3000, 1071 and \SI{382}{\second}. At $X=6600$~s it is 3.04, 1.76, 3.12 and 2.25~ms at the four temperatures in turn, reached after 6600, 2356, 841 and \SI{300}{\second}. The relaxation time thus varies 1.1- and 1.8-fold, not ordered in temperature, whereas the waiting time varies 7.8- and 22-fold. The fast relaxation follows the same rescaled time as $f$, and temperature sets how fast the state evolves rather than how fast the material relaxes in a given state.

\textbf{Collective bond number.} In scenario B of the main text, each repeat that changes state in the rate-limiting step adds its transition enthalpy to the activation enthalpy, so
\begin{align}
 N&\approx\frac{E_a}{\Delta H},\label{eq:si-n}\\
 \Delta H&=\Delta h\,M,\label{eq:si-dhrepeat}
\end{align}
where $\Delta h$ is the transition enthalpy per gram measured by calorimetry and $M=423.5$~g/mol is the mass of one VPAVG repeat. Calorimetry on heating in water exists only for chains longer than \VPAVG: 6.52~cal/g (27.3~J/g) for synthetic poly(VPAVG) retained by a 50~kDa dialysis membrane (more than 118 repeats),\cite{Luan1991-bp} 34~J/g for synthetic poly(VPAVG) of $M_n=74.8$~kDa (about 177 repeats)\cite{Reguera2003-ma} and 34~J/g for recombinant (VPAVG)$_{62}$.\cite{Ribeiro2009-bj} The transition enthalpy per gram of a related ELP, [(VPGVG)$_2$VPGEG(VPGVG)$_2$]$_n$ at pH 2.5, rises with chain length as 4.49, 7.82, 8.24, 8.87 and 9.03~J/g at 25, 45, 75, 150 and 225 pentapeptides, values read from Fig.~4 of Ref.~\citenum{Girotti2004-ma}. Writing $\Delta h_G(n)$ for that dependence, interpolated linearly in the number $n$ of pentapeptides, each long-chain value is scaled to 30 repeats as
\begin{equation}
 \Delta h_{30}=s\,\Delta h_\mathrm{long},\qquad s=\frac{\Delta h_G(30)}{\Delta h_G(n_\mathrm{long})},
 \label{eq:si-scale}
\end{equation}
with $n_\mathrm{long}$ the length of the calibrating chain, taken at its lower bound of 118 repeats for Luan \textit{et al.} The resulting values are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\hline
Calibration & $n_\mathrm{long}$ & $s$ & $\Delta h$ (J/g) & $\Delta H$ (kJ/mol) & $N$ (chains) \\
\hline
Luan \textit{et al.}, long chain & $>118$ & 1 & 27.3 & 11.55 & 270 (9.0) \\
Reguera \textit{et al.}, long chain & 177 & 1 & 34.0 & 14.40 & 216 (7.2) \\
Ribeiro \textit{et al.}, long chain & 62 & 1 & 34.0 & 14.40 & 216 (7.2) \\
Luan \textit{et al.}, scaled & & 0.619 & 16.9 & 7.15 & 436 (14.5) \\
Reguera \textit{et al.}, scaled & & 0.596 & 20.3 & 8.59 & 363 (12.1) \\
Ribeiro \textit{et al.}, scaled & & 0.661 & 22.5 & 9.51 & 327 (10.9) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The scaling assumes that poly(VPAVG) loses enthalpy with chain length as that ELP does, and the interpolation between 25 and 45 pentapeptides is coarse, so the main text quotes both the long-chain and the scaled values. In 0.5~M NaCl the enthalpy of poly(VPAVG) rises 1.09-fold, from 2.76 to 3.01~kcal/mol,\cite{Luan1991-bp} which would lower $N$ by 8\% at that concentration and by less at the 0.25~M of the buffer used here.

\textbf{Calorimetric kinetics.} Reguera \textit{et al.} obtained the activation energy of the calorimetric transition by isoconversional (Friedman) analysis.\cite{Reguera2003-ma} With $\alpha$ the fraction of the transition heat absorbed and $T_\alpha$ the temperature at which a run reaches it,
\begin{equation}
 \ln\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)_{\!\alpha}=\ln\!\left[A\,g(\alpha)\right]-\frac{E_a(\alpha)}{R\,T_\alpha},
 \label{eq:si-friedman}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{d}\alpha/\mathrm{d}t$ is the heat flow divided by the total transition heat, $g(\alpha)$ is an unknown function of the conversion and $R$ is the gas constant. Runs at heating rates of \SIrange{2}{30}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} reach the same $\alpha$ at different temperatures, and at fixed $\alpha$ the slope of $\ln(\mathrm{d}\alpha/\mathrm{d}t)$ against $1/T_\alpha$ gives $E_a(\alpha)$. Like the rescaling of Figure~\ref*{MT-fig:arrest}b, the analysis compares the rate of one process at the same stage at different temperatures, so the two activation energies are quantities of the same kind. For poly(VPAVG) on heating it gives 480~kJ/mol at the onset of the transition, 340~kJ/mol up to about 30\% conversion and 220~kJ/mol beyond 85\%, 6.5 to 14 times smaller than $E_a$. If that transition set the pace of arrest, $T_s=RT^2/E_a$ would be 1.6 to 3.4~K rather than 0.24~K.
