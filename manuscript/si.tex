\section{Recombinant Synthesis and Purification of \texorpdfstring{\VPAVG}{(VPAVG)30}}
\label{sec:synthesis}

\subsection{Materials}
Shrimp alkaline phosphatase, PfuUltra II Fusion HS DNA polymerase, GoTaq Green Master Mix, and T4 DNA ligase with PEG4000 additive were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Exonuclease I was purchased from New England Biolabs. CircLigase ssDNA ligase was purchased from Lucigen, and KOD Hot Start DNA polymerase was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. PCR products and plasmids were purified using Monarch PCR and DNA Cleanup kits and Monarch Plasmid Miniprep columns (New England Biolabs). DNA was recovered from agarose gels using the Monarch DNA Gel Extraction kit (New England Biolabs). Oligonucleotides and the 5$'$-phosphorylated synthetic template used for rolling-circle amplification were obtained from Integrated DNA Technologies. \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold cells were obtained from Stratagene.

\subsection{Gene construction}
The ELP gene was prepared by overlap-extension rolling-circle amplification (OERCA).\cite{Amiram2011-la} A single-stranded DNA template encoding five VPAVG repeats was circularized in a \SI{20}{\micro\liter} reaction containing \SI{10}{\pico\mole} template, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ CircLigase buffer, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{1}{\milli\mole\per\liter} ATP, \SI{1}{\micro\liter} of \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MnCl$_2$, and 100 U CircLigase. The reaction was incubated for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{60}{\degreeCelsius}, heat-inactivated for \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{80}{\degreeCelsius}, purified, and treated with exonuclease I for \SI{1}{\hour} at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to remove unreacted linear template.

OERCA was performed with 300 ng circular template, \SI{1.5}{\micro\liter} each of \SI{50}{\micro\mole\per\liter} primers A1 and A2 (Figure~\ref{fig:S1}a), \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of 70\% GC dNTP mix, \SI{2}{\micro\liter} of \SI{25}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$, \SI{10}{\micro\liter} of 10$\times$ PfuUltra II buffer, \SI{5}{\micro\liter} DMSO, and \SI{2}{\micro\liter} PfuUltra II polymerase in a total volume of \SI{100}{\micro\liter}. Cycling consisted of \SI{2}{\minute} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}; 30 cycles of \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{95}{\degreeCelsius}, \SI{20}{\second} at \SI{54}{\degreeCelsius}, and \SI{90}{\second} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}; and a final \SI{10}{\minute} at \SI{72}{\degreeCelsius}. Products above approximately 1.2 kb were isolated from a 0.8\% agarose gel.

Ligation-independent cloning adapters were introduced by PCR using primers B1 and B2 (Figure~\ref{fig:S1}a), and the products were inserted into the pMCSG53 vector.\cite{Aslanidis1990-lm,Eschenfeldt2009-bi} Constructs were transformed into \textit{E. coli} BL21(DE3)-Gold, screened for soluble expression, and sequence verified by Sanger sequencing. The selected construct encodes the amino-acid sequence \texttt{MHHHHHHSSGVDLGTENLYFQSNA(VPAVG)30}, comprising a cleavable N-terminal affinity/fusion sequence followed by 30 VPAVG repeats. That leader was kept rather than cleaved, because its single tyrosine is the only chromophore in the chain and the $A_{280}$ concentration measurement depends on it. Every measurement reported here is therefore on the tagged construct, and the leader accounts for 24 of the 174 residues and holds all eight charged residues.

\subsection{Protein expression and purification}
Fresh transformants were grown in Terrific Broth containing \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} MgSO$_4$ and \SI{300}{\micro\gram\per\milli\liter} ampicillin at \SI{37}{\degreeCelsius} to an optical density of 0.6--0.8 at 600 nm. Cultures were cooled to \SI{18}{\degreeCelsius}, induced with \SI{0.2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} IPTG, and grown overnight. Cells were harvested at $5000\times g$, resuspended in ice-cold lysis buffer (\SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, 5 vol\% glycerol, \SI{50}{\milli\mole\per\liter} 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfonic acid (HEPES) at pH 8.0, \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole, and \SI{10}{\milli\mole\per\liter} 2-mercaptoethanol), treated with \SI{1}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} lysozyme for \SI{10}{\minute}, sonicated, and clarified at $29{,}000\times g$.\cite{Makowska-Grzyska2014-cz}

The supernatant was applied to \SI{5}{\milli\liter} IMAC Sepharose High Performance resin, washed with lysis buffer, and eluted with lysis buffer containing \SI{500}{\milli\mole\per\liter} imidazole. Purification fractions were monitored by SDS-PAGE. The purified protein was exchanged into \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES at pH 8.0, \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl, and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} dithiothreitol (DTT) and concentrated using a 10 kDa molecular-weight-cutoff centrifugal concentrator. Protein concentration was determined by direct $A_{280}$ measurement on a NanoDrop spectrophotometer using the sequence-specific molar extinction coefficient applied during sample preparation (calculated molecular weight 15{,}461~Da and $\varepsilon_{280}=1490$~M$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-1}$, both for the tagged construct). The final concentration was approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter}; samples were stored at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius}, and freshly prepared protein was used for the X-ray measurements. The expression, solubility, and purity of the protein were evaluated by SDS-PAGE (Figure~\ref{fig:S1}b).

\section{Sample Environments and X-ray Measurement Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS at 12-ID-B}
Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS was performed at Advanced Photon Source (APS) beamline 12-ID-B. Two aliquots of \VPAVG{} at approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} in \SI{20}{\milli\mole\per\liter} HEPES (pH 8), \SI{250}{\milli\mole\per\liter} NaCl and \SI{2}{\milli\mole\per\liter} DTT, nominally the same concentration used for the 8-ID-I measurements, were sealed in \SI{2}{\milli\meter} quartz capillaries. The two beamtimes fell in different APS run cycles several months apart, and because the protein is purified fresh for each experiment, the 12-ID-B and 8-ID-I material came from separate preparations of the same construct. The aliquot designated Measurement underwent more than 10 heating/cooling cycles across the thermal transition, whereas the Reference aliquot remained refrigerated at \SI{4}{\degreeCelsius}. Both aliquots were subsequently measured at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} and \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} during APS run 2021-1. The \SI{13.3}{\kilo\electronvolt} beam had an approximate size of $100\times150~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ and an incident flux of approximately $5\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$. SAXS was recorded with an Eiger2 9M detector at an approximately \SI{2}{\meter} sample-to-detector distance, the same energy and camera length used for a published SAXS measurement at this beamline\cite{Xu2022-12IDB} and the same camera length as another\cite{Zheng2022-12IDB}, while WAXS was recorded with a Pilatus 300K detector at \SI{0.42}{\meter}. The Pilatus detector was mounted above the direct-beam path, allowing the transmitted beam to continue to the SAXS detector so that SAXS and WAXS were acquired simultaneously with a \SI{0.1}{\second} exposure. Buffer and capillary backgrounds were subtracted, and the SAXS and WAXS profiles were stitched over their common $Q$ interval. At each set point the capillary was held at the target temperature for at least \SI{10}{\minute} before the exposures were started, so each profile reports the structure present after at least that long at temperature rather than during the temperature change. The two experiments used different sample cells, thermal paths, and thermometry, and the time spent at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} before each 12-ID-B exposure is known only as a lower bound. The 12-ID-B \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} profiles are therefore kept off the isothermal waiting-time axis of the 8-ID-I experiment; they establish the low- and high-temperature structures and their reversibility, not the kinetics of assembly.

\subsection{Isothermal SA-XPCS at 8-ID-I}
\label{sec:isoxpcs}
Small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) was performed at APS 8-ID-I. Approximately \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} \VPAVG{} in the same buffer was loaded into an aluminum cell containing a \SI{3}{\milli\meter}-diameter, \SI{2}{\milli\meter}-thick liquid chamber sealed by \SI{0.127}{\milli\meter} polycarbonate windows (Figure~\ref{fig:S2}). The cell was inserted into an anodized-aluminum holder in one temperature zone of the Quantum Northwest 3$\times$3 multi-sample temperature control unit, the same stage used in Ref.~\citenum{Ozgulbas2023-oz}. Each zone is driven by a Peltier element and a resistive heater, and a calibrated resistance-temperature detector (RTD) embedded adjacent to the Peltier stage supplies the controller feedback and readout. The aluminum holder and aluminum cell provided direct conductive thermal coupling. A partially coherent \SI{10.94}{\kilo\electronvolt} X-ray beam with 0.03\% FWHM bandwidth and incident flux $1.2\times10^{10}$ photons s$^{-1}$ was focused to approximately $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$. Coherent scattering was recorded \SI{7.8}{\meter} downstream with a Rigaku XSPA-500k photon-counting detector comprising 1024$\times$512 pixels with a \SI{76}{\micro\meter} pixel size and a \SI{320}{\micro\meter}-thick silicon sensor. The detector was operated in 2-bit mode at a \SI{50}{\kilo\hertz} frame rate; each acquisition contained 100,000 frames, corresponding to \SI{20}{\micro\second} per frame and \SI{2}{\second} total duration. The multi-tau delay list is built from the recorded \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, and the longest accessible delay is \SI{1.638}{\second}.\cite{Zhang2021-jl}

For the isothermal experiment, the loaded sample was first held at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} for more than \SI{30}{\minute}. It was then heated to \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{5}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute}, followed by a slower ramp from \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} to the target temperature of \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute}. The two-stage protocol was used to reduce overshoot near the target temperature. The waiting time $t_w$ was set to zero when the calibrated RTD readout reached \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}; data collected during the preceding ramps were not included in the isothermal waiting-time series. From that point the measurement simply ran. Single \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions were taken one after another for the rest of the experiment, each at a previously unexposed position on a grid of sample positions, at a cadence of roughly \SI{6.3}{\second} per acquisition once stage motion and readout are included. A single acquisition is therefore a snapshot of a fresh piece of sample at a known $t_w$, and the series steps through increasing $t_w$ at that cadence. SAXS profiles were obtained by temporal and azimuthal averaging. Each reported elapsed time is a group average, not a single acquisition: consecutive \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions were averaged in contiguous run ranges of 63--200 acquisitions after cross-correlation outlier rejection, and the quoted $t_w$ is the acquisition time of the first run in the range. The five groups analyzed for $g_2$ begin at $t_w=5040$, 5970, 6596, 7222 and \SI{7863}{\second} and hold 150, 100, 100, 100 and 63 acquisitions, spanning 15.5, 10.4, 10.4, 10.7 and \SI{6.7}{\minute}; the last is short only because the measurement ended there. The $t_w=0$ SAXS profile of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a averages the first 200 acquisitions, over \SI{20.8}{\minute}. Within any one group the fitted fast fraction (Section~\ref{sec:xpcsfit}) changes by less than its own group-to-group step, so this averaging does not materially broaden the correlation functions. Correlation functions were extracted at these elapsed times once the low-$Q$ intensity provided sufficient photon statistics.

\section{12-ID-B SAXS/WAXS Analysis}
\label{sec:b12}
\subsection{Background subtraction and SAXS/WAXS merging}
The beamline-reduced \texttt{.avg} files contain $Q$, $I(Q)$ and a pointwise intensity uncertainty, the counting error $\sqrt{I}$. For each aliquot and temperature the capillary-plus-buffer profile was subtracted with a scale factor $\alpha$ of 1.00 for SAXS and the empirical 0.95 used in the original WAXS reduction, and the uncertainties were propagated through that subtraction as $\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}}$. The WAXS intensity and its uncertainty were then multiplied by the ratio of the mean SAXS and WAXS intensities over their common $Q$ interval, that ratio being treated as exact, and the two profiles were concatenated and sorted by $Q$.

Those propagated uncertainties enter one result. The Guinier fit of Section~\ref{sec:guinier} is weighted, each point carrying $1/\sigma_{\ln I}^{2}$ with $\sigma_{\ln I}=\sigma_I/I$, and they therefore set the quoted errors on $R_g$ and $I_0$. The low-$Q$ power-law fit and the two Gaussian peak fits are unweighted, so the exponent and the peak centers and widths do not depend on them. Neither the 0.95 nor the overlap ratio affects a reported result either: no absolute WAXS intensity is used anywhere in this work, and the centers and widths of the two peaks, and hence the $\beta$-sheet-like spacings, depend on neither.

\subsection{Low-\texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q} power law and WAXS peak fits}
For Figure~\ref{MT-fig:static} of the main text, the low-$Q$ slope was obtained by an unweighted linear fit of $\log_{10} I$ against $\log_{10} Q$ over $0.012<Q<0.040$~\Ainv{} for the Reference aliquot at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}. The two WAXS features were fitted independently to a Gaussian plus linear background over $0.48<Q<1.00$ and $1.18<Q<1.70$~\Ainv, respectively. The low-$Q$ exponent is reported as an exponent and is deliberately not converted into a fractal dimension. Two conditions set out by Teixeira\cite{Teixeira1988-jac} are not met. First, a mass-fractal dimension must satisfy $D<3$, whereas the fitted 12-ID-B slopes are $-3.17$ and $-3.15$; exponents between 3 and 4 belong instead to the surface-fractal form $Q^{-(6-D_{\mathrm{s}})}$, which that reference notes is not easily distinguished from the $Q^{-4}$ of a smooth interface. Second, a fractal description is expected to hold over at least one decade in $Q$, and the fit window $0.012<Q<0.040$~\Ainv{} spans 0.52 decades. The isothermal 8-ID-I exponents, $-2.45$ to $-2.68$, do fall below 3, so the two datasets sit on opposite sides of that boundary; this is one more reason they are treated as complementary rather than as replicate measurements of one state. The reduced profiles and scripts implementing the background subtraction, overlap scaling, Guinier analysis, power-law fit, and peak fits are available in the public repository identified in Section~\ref{sec:code}.

\subsection{Low-temperature unassembled state}
\label{sec:guinier}
The Reference profile at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius} contains a Guinier regime satisfying $Q_{\mathrm{max}}R_g=1.246$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S3}). A fit of $\ln I(Q)$ against $Q^2$, weighted by those propagated uncertainties, gives
\begin{equation}
 R_g=22.65\pm0.50~\text{\AA}.
 \end{equation}
For reference, the cross-$\beta$ pattern is reported at approximately 4.7--4.8~\AA{} along the fibril direction and between 8 and 11~\AA{} perpendicular to it.\cite{Sunde1997-jmb,Sawaya2007-nature} Our 8.8~\AA{} falls inside that second range and our 4.5~\AA{} sits just below the first.

The fitted size is molecular in scale and is consistent with a dispersed, unassembled low-temperature state rather than persistent mesoscale aggregates. Because the protein concentration is high, this value is reported as an apparent $R_g$; weak intermolecular structure-factor contributions cannot be excluded. Recovery of the same low-temperature profile after repeated cycling establishes that the large high-temperature assembly is no longer detectable after cooling.

\section{8-ID-I Absolute Scattering Cross Section}
\label{sec:b8saxs}
\subsection{Absolute scattering cross section}
For the 8-ID-I data, the differential scattering cross section was calculated as
\begin{equation}
 I(Q)=A_s I_s(Q)-A_b I_b(Q),
 \end{equation}
where $I_s(Q)$ and $I_b(Q)$ are the temporal-mean detector intensities from the sample and from its background, per pixel and per frame. The background is a buffer where one was measured and the empty cell of Section~\ref{sec:tdepbg} otherwise. The conversion factors were
\begin{equation}
 A_j=\frac{N_{\mathrm{f}}}{F_jT_jt_{\mathrm{exp}}L\Delta\Omega},\qquad j\in\{s,b\},
 \end{equation}
where $F_j$ is the incident photon flux, $T_j$ is the transmission, $N_{\mathrm{f}}=100{,}000$ is the number of frames, $t_{\mathrm{exp}}=\SI{2}{\second}$ is the total exposure time, so that $t_{\mathrm{exp}}/N_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the \SI{20}{\micro\second} frame time, $L=\SI{2}{\milli\meter}$ is the sample thickness, and $\Delta\Omega=(d_{\mathrm{pix}}/D)^2$ is the solid angle per detector pixel.\cite{Sheyfer2020-yb} Lengths are carried in millimeters throughout, so $A_jI_j$ is in mm$^{-1}$, and that is the quantity plotted in Figures~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a and \ref{fig:S4}b: no further scaling of any kind is applied between the calibration and the figure.

\subsection{Transmission and incident-flux calibration}
Sample transmission was obtained from ion chambers immediately upstream and downstream of the sample. Across the attenuation series, the transmission of the intervening air path was independent of beam intensity within uncertainty, with a mean value of $0.8679\pm0.0009$ (Figure~\ref{fig:S5}a; mean $\pm$ standard deviation). The upstream ion-chamber signal was converted to incident photon flux by calibration against a PIN diode positioned in the flight path upstream of the beamstop. The measured photon flux varied linearly with the upstream ion-chamber readout (Figure~\ref{fig:S5}b).

\section{8-ID-I SA-XPCS Correlation Analysis}
\label{sec:b8xpcs}
\subsection{Correlation functions and the fitted model}
The intensity autocorrelation function was calculated with a multi-tau algorithm,
\begin{equation}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t)=\frac{\langle I(Q,t)I(Q,t+\Delta t)\rangle_t}{\langle I(Q,t)\rangle_t^2}.
 \label{eq:si-g2}
 \end{equation}
\noindent The implementation is the 8-ID-I reduction workflow; its numerical treatment of the multi-tau scheme at these frame rates is described in detail elsewhere.\cite{Zhang2021-jl,Switalski2022-jsr}

The reduction produced 27 azimuthally averaged dynamic $Q$ bins of nominal width $9.9\times10^{-4}$~\Ainv{} spanning \SIrange{0.00359}{0.02937}{}~\Ainv, and 61 delay times from \SI{20}{\micro\second} to \SI{1.64}{\second}. The five lowest bins, centered at $Q=0.00359$, 0.00458, 0.00557, 0.00657 and 0.00756~\Ainv{} and corresponding to $2\pi/Q$ of approximately 175, 137, 113, 96 and \SI{83}{\nano\meter}, are analyzed here. The restriction is a signal-to-noise limit: the scattered intensity falls by more than an order of magnitude between the lowest bin and 0.00756~\Ainv, so above that bin the correlation function is dominated by counting noise. Figure~\ref{fig:S4}c shows the still-mobile limit of the two-step behavior directly.

The measured $g_2$ carry two decays separated by several decades in delay, and were parameterized through a normalized intermediate scattering function $g_1$ built from two stretched exponentials,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 g_2(Q,\Delta t,t_w)={}&1+\beta\left|g_1(Q,\Delta t,t_w)\right|^{2},\\
 g_1(Q,\Delta t,t_w)={}&f(Q,t_w)\exp\!\left[-\left(D_0(t_w)\,Q^{2}\,\Delta t\right)^{p_{\mathrm{fast}}}\right]\\
 &+\left[1-f(Q,t_w)\right]\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}(Q,t_w)}\right)^{p_{\mathrm{slow}}(t_w)}\right].
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:si-twomode}
 \end{equation}
The first line is the Siegert relation. The two-mode form on the second line is the same one used for thermoreversible nanoparticle gels, where the two-step decay is fitted as a linear sum of two stretched exponential modes with complementary amplitudes $a$ and $1-a$.\cite{Bahadur2019-ln} The same two-mode decomposition, with a fast decay and a slow stretched decay whose amplitude sets the intermediate plateau, describes ergodicity breaking in aging nanoclay suspensions.\cite{Chen2020-fn} Here $g_1$ is the normalized intermediate scattering function, $f$ is the fitted fraction of fast dynamics and $1-f$ its complement. $\beta$ is the instrumental speckle contrast, set by the transverse coherence of the beam and by the ratio of speckle size to pixel size. It is a property of the beamline and the detector rather than of the sample, is measured on a static reference, and is held fixed at 0.141 throughout (Figure~\ref{fig:S5}). $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is free at each $Q$ and each $t_w$. The fast relaxation time is not free, being tied to the diffusive form $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}(Q,t_w)=1/[D_0(t_w)Q^2]$, so one coefficient replaces five free times at each waiting time. The Kohlrausch--Williams--Watts exponents carry no $Q$ argument because they are shared across the five bins. $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is free at each waiting time rather than shared along a hold: these holds run from a mobile state into arrest, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ falls from about 1 to about 0.2 as they do, and a single shared value is rejected by an $F$ test at $p<10^{-16}$ in every loading that arrests. The decomposition is phenomenological: it describes the observed first decay and persistent plateau without assigning either mode to a unique molecular mechanism.

\subsection{Global fitting and uncertainties}
\label{sec:xpcsfit}
Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode} was fitted to all five $Q$ bins of one averaged group at once. $f$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are free in each bin; $D_0$, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ are single values shared across the bins of that group. Groups are fitted independently of one another, so nothing is shared along a hold. Fits minimized the error-weighted residuals $[g_{2,\mathrm{model}}-g_{2,\mathrm{data}}]/\sigma_{g_2}$ with \texttt{scipy.optimize.least\_squares}, using the pointwise $g_2$ uncertainties stored in the reduced files, so each delay enters with weight $1/\sigma_{g_2}^2$ and the noisy short delays count for little. Bounds were wide ($0\le f\le1$, $1\le\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}\le10^4$~s, $0.2\le p\le3$, $10^{2}\le D_0\le10^{10}$~\AA$^2$\,s$^{-1}$). Each fit is started from the minimum of a $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ profile so that it cannot settle in a solution where the fast term imitates the slow one. Reduced $\chi^2$ runs from 0.90 to 3.34 over the four loadings, and a group is kept only if it stays below 10. Starting values and the remaining numerical settings are in the code (Section~\ref{sec:code}).

Parameter uncertainties are the square roots of the diagonal of the covariance matrix, estimated as the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse of $J^{\mathrm{T}}J$ with $J$ the Jacobian of the error-weighted residuals at the solution; the pseudo-inverse is used so that a parameter refining to a bound does not leave the uncertainties of the remaining, well-constrained parameters undefined. The fast fraction reaches its lower bound of zero in the latest groups of the holds that arrest furthest. These are the error bars plotted in Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}c and in Figures~\ref{fig:S8}--\ref{fig:S8}.

The slow component remains only partially relaxed during the \SI{2}{\second} acquisition and frequently yields $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}>2$~s, so it is an effective extrapolation rather than a sampled terminal relaxation time. Conclusions about arrest therefore rest on directly observed features: the decreasing amplitude of the first decay, the rising plateau, and the systematic decrease in $f$. XPCS does not determine where the arrested regions are, $1-f$ is not a geometric volume fraction because scattering contributions are weighted by structure and contrast, and because the measurement does not reach infinite delay it is not the formal nonergodicity factor either. Panels (d)--(g) of Figures~\ref{fig:S8}--\ref{fig:S8} give the correlation functions and fits at the four additional $Q$ values: the same two-step shape and the same monotonic loss of the fast step appear at every measured $Q$, so the trend of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}c belongs to the whole \SIrange{83}{175}{\nano\meter} range and not to a single bin.

\subsection{Stretching exponents and the fast-mode scaling}
Both stretching exponents stay below unity. $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ runs from about 0.9 while a hold is still mobile to about 0.3 once it has arrested, and $p_{\mathrm{slow}}$ is free as well. That the breadth of the fast relaxation grows as the material locks up is itself a measurement rather than a modelling choice: a single $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ shared along a hold is rejected by an $F$ test at $p<10^{-16}$ in each of the three loadings that arrest, while the \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} loading, which does not arrest, gains nothing from freeing it. Stretched decays of this kind indicate a broad distribution of relaxation times rather than a single process, and are characteristic of protein and colloidal gels,\cite{Begam2021-fh,Bahadur2019-ln} although the direction of the departure from $p=1$ varies between systems: the egg-white gel of Ref.~\citenum{Begam2021-fh} relaxes with compressed rather than stretched exponentials, the signature of stress-driven rather than diffusive relaxation.

The fast relaxation occurs on approximately $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$~s and becomes faster with increasing $Q$. Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode} imposes the diffusive scaling $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}\propto Q^{-2}$ rather than fitting it, and a stretched $g_2$ remains compatible with diffusion provided $p$ does not depend on $Q$, which holds here by construction. Releasing the constraint, so that $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is free in every bin, returns a median $Q$ exponent of $-2.4$ over the 31 groups whose fast amplitude at the lowest $Q$ exceeds 0.15, with 80\% of them between $-2.6$ and $-1.4$. That release is also the general two-mode form of Ref.~\citenum{Bahadur2019-ln}, in which the fast time is free in every bin rather than carried by one coefficient. Refitting every group that way moves $f$ at the lowest $Q$ by at most 0.03, and by 0.01 or less in the groups where the fast mode has nearly gone, and moves the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:arrest} from 0.219 to 0.214~K, a shift of 2.2\% against a bin-to-bin spread of 5\%. The collapse therefore does not rest on the diffusive constraint. Where it is not, that is in the later groups of the holds that arrest furthest, the exponent collapses toward zero because $\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}$ stops being constrained once the fast decay has gone, not because the relaxation changes character. Those exponents bracket the imposed value of $-2$ but sit systematically steeper than it, so the diffusive form is reported here as an imposed approximation that the data are consistent with, and not as a fitted result. What is read from it is the fitted coefficient $D_0$ of Section~\ref{sec:tdepfit}, and the conclusions of this work rest on $f$ rather than on $D_0$: $f$ is set by the height of the plateau and stays measured to the end of every hold, whereas any fast-mode time scale needs a resolved decay. Freezing $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ at 0.5 for every group, against fitted values spanning 0.2 to 1.05, moves $f$ at the lowest $Q$ by 0.02 to 0.04 on a range of 0.04 to 1.00 and the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:arrest} by 3\%, so neither result depends on how the stretching is treated.

\section{Radiation-Damage Mitigation and Controls}
\label{sec:radiation}
\subsection{Fresh sample positions}
\label{sec:freshspots}
The grid of positions referred to in Section~\ref{sec:isoxpcs} was Cartesian, $100\times100$ points in the aluminum cell, with adjacent points separated by at least one beam diameter. Because no position was visited twice, cumulative dose cannot track the elapsed time of the isothermal experiment, so the trend in the fitted fast fraction cannot be a dose artifact.

\subsection{Flux-dependence control}
In addition, to show that there is no significant radiation-induced artifact, an independent dose-rate test was performed after the APS Upgrade using a \SI{20}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} \VPAVG{} sample. Every acquisition was taken at a stage-RTD readout of \SI{33.0}{\degreeCelsius}, above the transition temperature, so the control probed the assembled high-temperature state rather than the low-temperature dispersed one. Attenuation selected incident fluxes from $2.7\times10^{11}$ to $4.9\times10^{11}$ photons s$^{-1}$, approximately 23--41 times the flux used for the main experiment. The SAXS line shapes and $g_2$ decays showed no systematic flux dependence (Figure~\ref{fig:S7}). That control sample is five times more dilute than the \SI{100}{\milli\gram\per\milli\liter} material of the main experiment, and it was measured with the post-Upgrade beam. The main series is protected instead by the fresh position used for every acquisition (Section~\ref{sec:freshspots}).

\section{Macroscopic and Mesoscale Structural Reversibility}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Macroscopic reversibility}
Video~S1 provides a direct visual demonstration of thermal reversibility. Brief warming of a capillary containing \VPAVG{} produces turbidity within approximately \SI{5}{\second}, indicating structures large enough to scatter visible light. After removal of the heat source, the sample returns to optical transparency in approximately \SI{60}{\second}. This recovery is consistent with the overlap of the cycled Measurement and refrigerated Reference SAXS/WAXS profiles at \SI{10}{\degreeCelsius}. Optical transparency does not specify molecular structure, but independently confirms dissolution of the mesoscale assembly on cooling.

\subsection{The seven-cycle experiment}
\label{sec:thermalcycling}
The reversibility comparison of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:static} sets two separate 12-ID-B aliquots against each other. The test in Figure~\ref{fig:S4} is more direct: one aliquot at 8-ID-I, taken through seven consecutive cycles and compared with itself, so that any difference between cycles can only be irreversibility. The aliquot is not the loading used for the isothermal experiment, but comes from the same stock and was measured two days apart in the same cell position of the same holder, with the cell reloaded in between. Each cycle held the sample at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, ramped to \SI{34}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{1.01}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} over 270 consecutive \SI{2}{\second} acquisitions, and returned to \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, taking \SI{5.8}{\hour} in total. No acquisitions were taken during cooling, so the cooling segments of Figure~\ref{fig:S4}a are a projection from the fixed control protocol rather than a measurement; every solid segment is measured, once per acquisition, from the RTD of the stage zone holding the cell.

\subsection{Cycle-to-cycle repeatability}
\label{sec:cycling}
Three states are sampled in every cycle: \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, and two windows near the top of each ramp at \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} and \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius}. Averaged over $0.004<Q<0.008$~\Ainv, the seven cycles agree to a relative standard deviation of 9\% at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} and 5\% at \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius}, with no drift from the first cycle to the seventh. At \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} the spread is 33\%, but that reflects where on the ramp each cycle happened to be sampled rather than a difference between cycles: the windows fall between \SI{31.62}{\degreeCelsius} and \SI{32.48}{\degreeCelsius}, which is on the steep part of the transition. Regressing the band-averaged $\ln I$ of each cycle on that cycle's mean window temperature gives $\mathrm{d}\ln I/\mathrm{d}T=\SI{0.95}{\per\degreeCelsius}$ with $r=0.974$, and the spread taken about that line falls to 7\%. The same regression at \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius}, past the steep part, takes 5\% to 1\%. The cycles therefore repeat to better than 10\% at every state once the sampling temperature is accounted for.

\subsection{Dynamics at the cycled states}
The correlation functions say what state the sample was in when each was taken (Figure~\ref{fig:S4}c). At \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} the correlation function shows no resolvable decay anywhere in the accessible window, from $10^{-2}$ to \SI{2}{\second}, in any of the seven cycles: the sample was arrested on every heating. At \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} it decays throughout that same window, so the assembly is still mobile \SI{1.9}{\degreeCelsius} lower. The dashed line in that panel is the measured correlation level of a static reference recorded in the same beamtime, read at the same $Q$ bin. A static sample cannot relax, so its level is the instrumental contrast, and showing it as a curve rather than as a fitted number keeps the comparison between two measurements. The \SI{33.8}{\degreeCelsius} correlation functions sit just below it and the \SI{31.9}{\degreeCelsius} ones fall well away from it. A contrast value is needed to fit a correlation function, which is why the holds of Section~\ref{sec:tdep} carry their own calibration; it is not needed to judge whether a sample has arrested, which is what this panel does, so no arrested fraction is quoted from it. No correlation function is shown at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, where the coherent signal at the lowest $Q$ is too weak for any contrast to be resolved.

\subsection{Cold-state artifacts and the outlier cut}
\label{sec:coldstate}
Two features of the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} state need comment. First, its profile still rises toward low $Q$ instead of reaching the flat Guinier plateau of a fully dispersed solution. Mis-subtracted solvent does not account for it: the buffer contributes only about 35\% of the signal at the lowest $Q$, so rescaling it to remove the rise would subtract most of the low-$Q$ signal and would degrade the cycle-to-cycle agreement rather than improve it. Second, the cold state is where transient scatterers appear. Taking every acquisition as recorded, the intensity at $Q=0.0035$~\Ainv{} within a single \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} group is strongly right-skewed, while the same acquisitions at $Q=0.03$~\Ainv{} are symmetric at ordinary counting statistics; a long positive tail confined to low $Q$ is the signature of a large object crossing the $10\times10~\mu\mathrm{m}^{2}$ beam during a minority of acquisitions. The cross-correlation cut does not catch them, because it compares curve shape over the whole $Q$ range and a curve anomalous in three low-$Q$ bins alone stays close to the group mean by that measure.

A second, robust cut removes them, and is applied to every group in the paper on identical terms with no per-group list. For each acquisition the mean of $I(Q)$ over $0.004<Q<0.008$~\Ainv{} is compared with the group median, and the acquisition is rejected if it exceeds the median by more than three robust standard deviations, the scale being $1.4826\times$ the median absolute deviation and both statistics iterated to convergence. The test is one-sided because a transient scatterer only adds intensity. It removes 35 of the 1749 acquisitions surviving the cross-correlation cut, 2.0\%: 4, 2, 1, 11, 4, 1 and 1 from the seven \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} groups, 2 and 7 from the two buffer groups, and none at all from any of the fourteen high-temperature windows, which is consistent with the skew being confined to the cold state. Elsewhere it is nearly inert, removing two acquisitions of the isothermal series, shifting one absolute-scale coefficient by 0.03\% and leaving every fitted correlation-function parameter unchanged. Its effect here is reported rather than absorbed: the cycle-to-cycle spread at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} falls from 13\% to 9\%, and what remains is a mild rise over the last two cycles rather than one anomalous group. The threshold is not tuned, the same acquisitions being selected for any value between 2.5 and 3 and for two other choices of band.

One difference between the two loadings survives all of this and is a property of the material rather than of a few acquisitions: the cycling loading has $I(0.004)/I(0.02)=4.0$ against 2.7 for the isothermal loading, and transmits 0.305 against 0.333. It was the less completely dissolved of the two.

\section{Temperature Dependence of Arrest}
\label{sec:tdep}
\subsection{Measurements and reduction}
\label{sec:tdepdata}
Four further loadings of \VPAVG{} were measured at 8-ID-I in October 2021, before the isothermal series, with the same beam energy, detector, detector distance and cell design (Section~\ref{sec:isoxpcs}). They are the same construct as the isothermal loading, from a separate preparation. After a reference measurement at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius}, each loading was heated from \SI{20}{\degreeCelsius} at \SI{0.1}{\degreeCelsius\per\minute} to its final temperature and held there: loading D8 at \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius}, D6 at \SI{28.25}{\degreeCelsius}, D7 at \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius} and D9 at \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}. The temperatures are the readings of the stage RTD, and $t_w=0$ is the first acquisition with the RTD at its target. Acquisitions of 100,000 frames ran at fresh positions through the ramp and then for 7100, 8500, 3900 and \SI{2200}{\second} of the hold, respectively.

The 2021 Data Exchange records were converted to the NeXus format with the incident- and transmitted-beam monitors and the ring current carried across unchanged, and the frames were correlated with the same software as the isothermal series. Consecutive acquisitions were averaged in groups of 100, about \SI{10}{\minute} each, with one group boundary at $t_w=0$, through the same two outlier cuts that every other group in this work receives (Section~\ref{sec:coldstate}). One detector pixel read a constant 1.5 photons per frame in the measurements of 17 October and is masked in the $Q$ map the series is correlated against. It lay at $Q=0.0214$~\Ainv, outside the five $Q$ bins of the correlation analysis; masking it left every fitted quantity unchanged.

\subsection{Absolute scale, background and contrast}
\label{sec:tdepbg}
The beamtime used the same ion chambers as the isothermal series, so $I(Q)$ is put on the absolute scale of Section~\ref{sec:b8saxs}, each group with its own monitor readings. The four loadings transmit 0.383 to 0.389, against 0.384 to 0.390 for the isothermal loading. The background is a separate measurement of the sample cell, subtracted on the absolute scale exactly as the buffer is for the isothermal series. Six candidate measurements are available, four of them recorded on the same day as the loadings. They are identified here by what they transmit and what they scatter rather than by the labels entered at the beamline, which do not reliably distinguish a cell left empty from one holding buffer. Four of the six recorded a transmitted-beam monitor. One of those passes 0.94 of the beam and the other three pass 1.00, the same as the open path, so one has of order 6\% of attenuating material in the beam and the others have none that the chambers resolve. None passes anything near 0.41, which is what a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} column of buffer behind the same material would give, so none of them holds a liquid column of the sample thickness. The measurement that passes 0.94 is accordingly the only one of the six that carries scattering from material in the beam path, and that is what the subtraction needs. Fitted by the procedure below, the three that pass 1.00 need a coefficient between 3 and 5 and still leave the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} references 7 to 28\% from flat at best, against 3 to 5\% for the one that passes 0.94, with the residual peaking near 0.008~\Ainv. Averaging the three together does not improve on the best single one, so what they lack is shape and not counting statistics. Whether any of these measurements held buffer changes little over this $Q$ range: the absorption difference is taken up by the coefficient, and the scattering of a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} water column is about 0.0016~mm$^{-1}$, some 6\% of the plateau and flat in $Q$, so it shifts the level rather than the shape.

How much of that background to subtract is set by the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} state, which is dissolved and whose profile should fall smoothly. Each reference is reduced to the median in each of nine logarithmic $Q$ bins between 0.0036 and 0.0305~\Ainv, and two numbers describe the result: the rise, its lowest bin divided by its highest, and the largest fractional increase from one bin to the next going up in $Q$, which is zero when the profile never turns back up. The coefficient trades one against the other and nothing in these data escapes that trade. At $\alpha=0.865$, the value that makes the references flattest, the rise is 1.0 but the profile dips 6 to 8\% near 0.004 to 0.005~\Ainv, and a dip is a subtraction artefact rather than anything the sample does. Removing the dip altogether takes $\alpha=0.60$, which leaves a two-fold climb toward low $Q$ in its place. We use $\alpha=0.80$ for all four loadings. The rise is then 1.26 to 1.45 and the profile turns back up by at most 4\%, below the scatter of the individual points, and the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} references sit at 0.026 to 0.028~mm$^{-1}$ over 0.006 to 0.03~\Ainv.

Figures~\ref{fig:S8}--\ref{fig:S11} show $I(Q)$ from the lowest fitted $Q$ bin outward. Below that bin the points sit on the edge of the beamstop shadow, where the background and the sample no longer share a profile. No coefficient reproduces the shape of the isothermal series' \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference in Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a of the main text, which falls 3.25-fold between 0.004 and 0.029~\Ainv{} with its log-log slope steepening from $-0.49$ to $-1.07$, the roll-over of a form factor; the 2021-3 references keep the opposite curvature at every coefficient. Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs}a subtracts a buffer measured in the same cell, which takes windows, solvent and parasitic scattering off together. None of this beamtime's background measurements holds a buffer column, as the transmissions above show, and at 0.004~\Ainv{} the background is 4.7 times the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} signal, so subtracting enough to remove the instrumental tail removes the sample's own low-$Q$ signal with it. A mismatch of the $Q$ scale between the two was tested as the cause and rejected: flattening the residual that way takes a 6\% stretch of the background $Q$ axis and improves the agreement by half a percentage point. The assembling and arrested groups are 40 to 800 times the background over the same range, so this limit bears on the \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} reference alone.

Loading D8 was measured at \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} twice, before and after its hold, and Figure~\ref{fig:S8} shows the later measurement. The earlier one was the first measurement of the beamtime and is not a dissolved state: it carries a $Q^{-2.7}$ excess at low $Q$ that falls to 4\% of the plateau above 0.01~\Ainv, and it fits $\alpha=1.15$ against 0.85 to 0.88 for every other reference. The later measurement is the same loading in the same cell, returned to \SI{6}{\degreeCelsius} after the \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} hold; it is flat and fits $\alpha=0.860$. The excess is therefore undissolved material in the earlier measurement rather than a property of the subtraction.

The speckle contrast was measured on a static reference recorded in the same beamtime, as the flat level of its correlation function through the Siegert relation that opens Equation~\ref{eq:si-twomode}. It is \emph{not} taken over the five bins the correlation analysis fits, where that reference reads 0.1226 to 0.1390: the standard is scratched, and its static parasitic scattering depresses the apparent contrast most strongly at low $Q$. $\beta$ is instead read from the plateau the same reference reaches between 0.0085 and 0.0165~\Ainv, giving the single value $\beta=0.141$ used for every bin and every group. Figure~\ref{fig:S6} shows both the suppression and the plateau. Using the fitted bins instead would understate $\beta$ by 5\%, which returns too large an $f$: the arrested fractions quoted below would rise from 0.93 to 0.96, while the ordering of $f$ in $Q$ and the scaling temperature of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:arrest} would be unchanged.

\subsection{Fitting}
\label{sec:tdepfit}
Equation~\ref{MT-eq:twomode} of the main text was fitted to the five lowest $Q$ bins, 0.00359 to 0.00756~\Ainv{} (175 to \SI{83}{\nano\meter}), with $\beta$ fixed. A group is fitted when its correlation function is no noisier than the noisiest group fitted in the isothermal series: the median $g_2$ uncertainty at the lowest $Q$ between $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-2}$~s may not exceed 0.016. Two choices differ from the isothermal series, and both follow from what the holds contain. First, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is fitted in each group. The holds run from a largely mobile state, where $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ is 0.55 to 1.00, into arrest, where it is 0.20 to 0.51, and a single value shared across a hold is rejected by an $F$ test at $p<10^{-15}$ in every loading. Each fit starts from the minimum of a $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ profile, so that it cannot settle in a solution in which the fast term imitates the slow one. Second, the rate of the fast mode is reported as the fitted coefficient $D_0$ itself. No mean or median of the stretched decay is formed, because either one is dominated by the tail of the distribution and moves strongly with $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$. Reduced $\chi^2$ is 0.90 to 3.3. Early in arrest $\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}$ reaches its upper bound of $10^4$~s in 17 of 170 bin fits, where the slow mode does not decay within the acquisition; $f$ is set by the height of the plateau and is unaffected.

Fixing $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ would remove one parameter per group, and the data were tested against it. At $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=1$, where the fast mode is a single exponential, the mean reduced $\chi^2$ of the \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} hold rises only from 1.19 to 1.41, so a mobile sample does relax exponentially. The three holds that arrest rise from 1.75, 1.89 and 1.64 to 8.25, 9.56 and 7.22, and the fitted curve crosses the data rather than following it. Intermediate fixed values do not recover the fit: averaged over all 34 groups, $p_{\mathrm{fast}}=1$ gives 6.59, 0.7 gives 3.73 and 0.5 gives 2.44, against 2.31 for the best single value shared within each hold and 1.62 with $p_{\mathrm{fast}}$ fitted per group. The broadening of the fast relaxation is therefore a feature of the data in the arresting holds rather than a free parameter absorbing noise.

\subsection{Results}
Figures~\ref{fig:S8}--\ref{fig:S11} show the four loadings in the format of Figure~\ref{MT-fig:xpcs} of the main text, with the correlation functions at the four further $Q$ values in the format of Figure~\ref{fig:S9}. Assembly begins before any target is reached. Averaged over $0.004<Q<0.008$~\Ainv, the scattered intensity rises on the ramp to 2.3 to 3.6 times its level at 20 to \SI{22}{\degreeCelsius} by \SI{27.5}{\degreeCelsius}, in every loading.

At \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} the sample assembles without arresting. The same low-$Q$ intensity grows 22-fold over the hold, to 6.2~mm$^{-1}$, while $f$ stays at 0.89 or above at every $Q$ for \SI{6500}{\second}.

At the three higher temperatures the sample arrests, and the rate rises steeply with temperature. $f$ at the lowest $Q$ falls to 0.5 after 5810, 1930 and \SI{540}{\second} of hold at 28.25, 28.50 and \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}. Measured instead from the moment the RTD passed \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius}, which removes the different ramp times, and taking each group at its midpoint, the times are 6240, 2520 and \SI{1280}{\second}, a factor of five over \SI{0.5}{\degreeCelsius}. In every group in which arrest is under way ($f<0.85$ at the lowest $Q$), $f$ increases monotonically with $Q$: 7 of 7, 7 of 7 and 4 of 4 groups. Figure~\ref{MT-fig:arrest} puts $f$ at the largest length scale for all four holds on one axis. At \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius} the last group gives $f=0.07$ at \SI{175}{\nano\meter} and 0.16 at \SI{83}{\nano\meter}, close to the 0.00 and 0.12 of the isothermal series at its last waiting time. Arrest at \SI{28.25}{\degreeCelsius} here proceeds on the time scale of the isothermal series at \SI{30}{\degreeCelsius}.

The structure does not set the arrest. At \SI{28.25}{\degreeCelsius} the first group in which arrest is under way ($t_w=\SI{4140}{\second}$) has an $I(Q)$ between 0.88 and 1.11 times the final \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} profile at every $Q$ from 0.004 to 0.028~\Ainv. At \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius} ($t_w=\SI{6}{\second}$) the ratio is 0.82 to 0.92. That \SI{28.00}{\degreeCelsius} profile stays mobile. At \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius} arrest sets in at 0.48 to 0.69 of it. Once under way, arrest proceeds while the low-$Q$ intensity keeps rising, by a factor of 1.6 to 4.0 between the first group in which arrest is under way and the last.

The mobile population slows before arrest and then changes far less than the amplitude does. While $f$ at the lowest $Q$ stays at 0.7 or above, $D_0$ falls 2.7-, 3.8- and 3.4-fold at 28.00, 28.25 and \SI{28.50}{\degreeCelsius}. Below 0.7 it varies by factors of 1.7, 1.9 and 3.0 at 28.25, 28.50 and \SI{28.75}{\degreeCelsius}, while $1-f$ at the lowest $Q$ rises from 0.33, 0.46 and 0.53 to 0.86, 0.93 and 0.84. The isothermal series lies entirely in this late stage: its first fitted waiting time already has $1-f$ of 0.6 or more at the lowest $Q$.

  \caption{The speckle contrast of the 2021-3 beamtime, and why it is not read off the bins the correlation analysis fits. (a) The correlation function of the static reference at the lowest fitted $Q$ and inside the plateau. A static sample cannot relax, so each curve is flat and its level, drawn as a solid line, is $1+\beta$ at that $Q$. (b) $\beta$ against $Q$ over every dynamic bin. Contrast is set by the beam optics and the detector and is near enough independent of $Q$ over this range, so a clean reference returns a flat line. This one does not below 0.0085~\Ainv: it climbs 12\% from 0.1226 to the plateau. The reference is a glassy carbon standard whose surface scratches scatter statically, adding to $g_2$ without fluctuating and so depressing the apparent contrast most where that scattering is brightest. $\beta$ is therefore taken from the shaded plateau, 0.0085--0.0165~\Ainv, and applied to every bin as the single value $\beta=0.141$. The band stops short of a detector module gap at 0.0175--0.0185~\Ainv{} and of the bin at 0.0214~\Ainv{} holding the stuck pixel of Section~\ref{sec:tdepdata}, both marked. Taking $\beta$ from the fitted bins instead would understate it by 5\%, which returns too large an $f$ and so too small an arrested fraction; the ordering of $f$ in $Q$ is unaffected.}
