{\large\bfseries Significance Statement}

\vspace{0.6em}

{\footnotesize\itshape
Temperature, Not Structure, Sets Dynamic Arrest in an Elastin-like Protein
Polymer \\
Ozgulbas, Chu, Lavens, Marks, Du, Zuo, Irvin, Ma, Seifert, Lee, Vescovi,
Foster, Mateevitsi, Jensen, Akins, Ramanathan, Babnigg and Zhang \\
Physical Sciences: Applied Physical Sciences; secondary, Biophysics and
Computational Biology}

\vspace{1.2em}

Whether a soft material has solidified is usually inferred from its structure:
a scattering pattern that has grown enough is read as a gel. We show that this
inference can fail. In a designed protein polymer, samples held within a degree
of one another develop nearly the same structure, yet only the warmer ones stop
moving. Seeing this needed X-ray speckle framed at fifty thousand images per
second, because the motion reporting on arrest is millisecond-scale, below what
conventional detectors reach. Arrest spreads region by region, largest scales
first, and one rescaling of waiting time collapses every sample onto a single
pathway whose temperature scale is a fifth of a degree, too sharp for single
contacts to set.
