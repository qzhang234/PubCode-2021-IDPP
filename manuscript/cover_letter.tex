Dear Editors:

Please consider the enclosed Letter for publication in \textit{Nano Letters}: ``Mesoscale Arrest without Progressive Slowing in a Protein Polymer with Reversible $\beta$-Sheet-like Order.''

\textbf{Corresponding authors.} Qingteng Zhang \mbox{(\href{mailto:qzhang234@anl.gov}{qzhang234@anl.gov})}, Arvind Ramanathan \mbox{(\href{mailto:ramanathana@anl.gov}{ramanathana@anl.gov})}, and Gyorgy Babnigg \mbox{(\href{mailto:gbabnigg@anl.gov}{gbabnigg@anl.gov})}.

\textbf{Significance.} Dynamic arrest in a soft material is usually described as a progressive slowing of motion. In the sequence-defined elastin-like polypeptide (VPAVG)$_{30}$ it proceeds differently. Small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) at 30~$^\circ$C resolves a two-step correlation decay whose fast-decay fraction falls from $f=0.42$--0.71 to $f=0.01$--0.13 over roughly 47~min, while the fast relaxation time shows no systematic drift and keeps a near-diffusive $Q$ dependence. Material leaves the mobile population without slowing beforehand, and it does so at the largest length scales first: $f$ rises monotonically with $Q$ at every elapsed time, by nine standard deviations or more end to end across 76--167~nm. Arrest therefore proceeds by local conversion rather than bulk stiffening, which implies that a partially arrested protein network still contains fully mobile regions. Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS on the same construct resolves a reversible transition to a hierarchical assembly carrying $\beta$-sheet-like peaks at 1.38 and 0.72~\AA$^{-1}$ that vanish on cooling, with seven consecutive 6--34~$^\circ$C cycles recovering the low-temperature profile to within 9\%.

\textbf{Novelty.} Prior work on gel-forming ELPs established arrested phase separation and network morphology from time-averaged scattering and rheology. The dynamical signature reported here, a diffusive fast mode whose time scale plateaus while its amplitude declines, has also been observed in a thermoreversible nanoparticle gel [D. Bahadur \textit{et al.}, \textit{J. Chem. Phys.} \textbf{151}, 104902 (2019)], and the manuscript cites that agreement rather than claiming the phenomenology as new. What is new is the length-scale decomposition. Fitting the fast fraction separately in each $Q$ bin shows that arrest is ordered by length scale, reaching 167~nm before 76~nm at every elapsed time, and follows the transfer to near-completion at $f=0.01$. That decomposition requires microsecond framing: with the fast relaxation time between 0.1 and 1.6~ms, a kilohertz acquisition returns only the slow component. We are equally explicit about the limits. The structural and dynamical observations come from separate experiments, so no causal link is claimed. The $\beta$-sheet assignment rests on WAXS peak positions rather than spectroscopy. The 1.6~s maximum delay bounds a fraction arrested on that scale, not an infinite-time nonergodicity factor. The isothermal series comes from one loaded sample. All four are stated in the manuscript, and the ordered contacts are called plausible rather than established junctions.

\textbf{Impact and urgency.} The work spans sequence-defined nanomaterials, protein self-assembly, nonequilibrium soft matter, and coherent X-ray characterization, and gives a nanoscale structure--dynamics framework for responsive protein materials whose $\beta$-sheet-like order switches thermally without permanent aggregation. Because the behavior is sequence-encoded, the strategy transfers to other programmable protein polymers. The result rests on a capability that is not widely available. The XSPA-500k at 8-ID-I frames at 50~kHz, among the fastest in routine operation at a storage ring. The fast relaxation reported here, 0.1 to 1.6~ms, would fall at or below the shortest delay of a conventional kilohertz acquisition, and the amplitude transfer that is the paper's central result would not be visible at all. High-speed detectors and upgraded storage rings have only recently made this practical on weakly scattering biological samples, so a concise, carefully bounded demonstration is timely. The reduced data and all analysis and figure-generation code are already public on GitHub (\href{https://github.com/qzhang234/PubCode-2021-IDPP}{qzhang234/PubCode-2021-IDPP}).

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors. The authors declare no competing financial interest; AI-tool use is disclosed in the Acknowledgments. Thank you for your consideration.
