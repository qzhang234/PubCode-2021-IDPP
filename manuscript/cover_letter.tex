Dear Editors:

Please consider the enclosed Letter for publication in \textit{Nano Letters}: ``Reversible $\beta$-Sheet-like Ordering and XPCS-Resolved Dynamic Arrest during Elastin-like Polypeptide Phase Separation.''

\textbf{Corresponding authors.} Qingteng Zhang \mbox{(\href{mailto:qzhang234@anl.gov}{qzhang234@anl.gov})}, Arvind Ramanathan \mbox{(\href{mailto:ramanathana@anl.gov}{ramanathana@anl.gov})}, and Gyorgy Babnigg \mbox{(\href{mailto:gbabnigg@anl.gov}{gbabnigg@anl.gov})}.

\textbf{Significance.} Amyloid-like $\beta$-sheet packing is usually taken as a route to permanent aggregation; in the sequence-defined elastin-like polypeptide (VPAVG)$_{30}$ it need not be. Temperature-dependent SAXS/WAXS resolves a reversible transition from a dispersed state at 10~$^\circ$C to a hierarchical assembly at 30~$^\circ$C, with an $I(Q)\propto Q^{-3.2}$ low-$Q$ response and peaks at 1.38 and 0.72~\AA$^{-1}$ compatible with $\beta$-sheet-like packing. Both signatures vanish on cooling, and seven consecutive 6--34~$^\circ$C cycles recover the low-temperature profile to within 9\%. In a separate isothermal experiment, small-angle X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (SA-XPCS) at 30~$^\circ$C resolves a two-step correlation decay whose fast-decay fraction falls from $f=0.42$--0.71 to $f=0.00$--0.13 over roughly 47~min at every measured $Q$ (76--167~nm). Thermally labile local order therefore coexists with mesoscale dynamics that are progressively arrested, identifying $\beta$-sheet-like contacts as candidate switchable physical junctions rather than a commitment to irreversible aggregation.

\textbf{Novelty.} Prior work on gel-forming ELPs established arrested phase separation and network morphology from time-averaged scattering and rheology. This Letter adds the complementary dynamical measurement, asking whether local order stays reversible while the assembly loses mobility. Correlating 100{,}000 frames per 2~s acquisition at 50~kHz, each at a fresh sample position, separates the fast and slow populations and follows the transfer of weight between them --- behavior invisible in the time-averaged SAXS profile, which over the same interval only rises smoothly. To our knowledge this is the first report resolving reversible $\beta$-sheet-like ordering and progressive mesoscale arrest in the same sequence-defined protein polymer. We are equally explicit about the limits: the structural and dynamical observations come from separate experiments, so no causal link is claimed; the $\beta$-sheet assignment rests on WAXS peak positions, not spectroscopy; the 1.6~s maximum delay bounds a fraction arrested on that scale, not an infinite-time nonergodicity factor; and the isothermal series comes from one loaded sample. All four are stated in the manuscript, and the ordered contacts are called plausible rather than established junctions.

\textbf{Impact and urgency.} The work spans sequence-defined nanomaterials, protein self-assembly, nonequilibrium soft matter, and coherent X-ray characterization, and gives a nanoscale structure--dynamics framework for responsive protein materials whose $\beta$-sheet-like order switches thermally without permanent aggregation. Because the behavior is sequence-encoded, the strategy transfers to other programmable protein polymers. High-speed detectors and upgraded storage rings have only recently made XPCS at these count rates practical on weakly scattering biological samples, so a concise, carefully bounded demonstration is timely. The reduced data and all analysis and figure-generation code are already public on GitHub (\href{https://github.com/qzhang234/PubCode-2021-IDPP}{qzhang234/PubCode-2021-IDPP}).

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors. The authors declare no competing financial interest; AI-tool use is disclosed in the Acknowledgments. Thank you for your consideration.
